## 1. Resolved in this repo's favour — with a stronger citation

**Audit claim:** *"'Current law does not impose a separate designation deadline.' I could not confirm
this… If a designation cutoff exists earlier than 2029, it changes the sequencing on every program."*

**Disposition: CONFIRMED CORRECT.** Re-verified against the US Code amendment notes (statute text was
already on disk at `360ff_prelim.html`). The prior regime *did* have two dates. The 2026 amendment did
not collapse them — it **deleted the conditional clause outright**:

> **2026** — Subsec. (b). Pub. L. 119–75, § 6604(a), substituted **"September 30, 2029."** for
> *"December 20, 2024, unless the rare pediatric disease product application— (A) is for a drug that,
> not later than December 20, 2024, is **designated** under subsection (d)…; and (B) is, not later
> than September 30, 2026, **approved** under section 355(b)(1)…"*

Current § 360ff(b)(5) reads simply: *"The Secretary may not award any priority review vouchers under
paragraph (1) after September 30, 2029."* Both the designation-by prong and the separate approval-by
prong were struck together. **The single binding constraint is award (approval) by 2029-09-30.**

*Bonus from the same notes:* **Pub. L. 117-9 (2021)** is what replaced *"no active ingredient
(including any ester or salt)"* with the **active moiety** test. Pre-2021 commentary on § 360ff
eligibility is unreliable.

## 4. The most important part of the audit — the untested assumptions

> *"Section 5 asserts alignment rather than testing it. The claims the inventory does not actually
> test are: whether the usable drug-disease pool is large enough to support six to ten parallel
> programs, whether pediatric patient recruitment is feasible at that count simultaneously, and
> whether any program can clear designation plus trial plus NDA plus approval inside the window to
> September 2029."*

**This is correct and it applies to this repo too.** Everything here is a *source map* — it establishes
that data exists and how to reach it. It does not test whether the business closes. The three
load-bearing questions are unanswered:

1. **Pool size.** How many never-approved-in-US active moieties exist with credible published evidence
   in a rare pediatric disease? Funnel A is the only PRV-eligible funnel, so if this number is single
   digits the "6–10 parallel programs" plan does not survive contact.
2. **Recruitment feasibility.** ~20 patients per trial × 6–10 simultaneous programs, in diseases with
   a few hundred US patients each, competing against each other for sites and families.
3. **Timeline.** Now sharper given § 1 above: the *only* deadline is **approval by 2029-09-30**, which
   is **~3.1 years from today**. That must absorb IND, trial conduct, NDA preparation, the 60-day
   filing review, and a 6-month priority (or 10-month standard) review clock. Working backwards, an
   NDA must be submitted by roughly **mid-2028** — leaving under two years for trial execution.

These are being addressed as a dedicated feasibility analysis rather than more source mapping.
