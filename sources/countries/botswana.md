### 3.3 Four-pathway scheme is DRAFT only

The **current in-force** framework has no separate codified "abridged" or "verification" pathway — reg. 3 of S.I. 163/2019 provides one application route (Form 1 + CTD Form 2 + sample); abridgement arises *operationally* through the reliance guideline.

The **draft** Human Medicines Regulations 2026, reg. 6(1), would codify four pathways:
- **(a) Full Evaluation** — NCEs, new biological entities, products not previously approved by any recognised authority, first-in-class, novel delivery systems.
- **(b) Abridged Evaluation** — "products approved by a Stringent Regulatory Authority, WHO Prequalified products, products approved through recognised regional harmonisation initiatives, and **generic products where the reference medicine is registered in Botswana**".
- **(c) Verification** — "products approved by **two or more** Stringent Regulatory Authorities and products on the **WHO Essential Medicines List** approved by recognised regulatory authorities".
- **(d) Notification** — low-risk products per guidelines.

reg. 6(5): in applying Abridged and Verification, the Authority may rely on assessments/inspections/decisions of recognised authorities under **s.109 of the Act**, "while retaining sovereign decision-making authority". Draft **Schedule 3**: Major variations 90 / Minor 60 / Notifications 30 **working days**; all *registration* pathways say only **"As per guidelines"**.

Statutory reliance hooks in MRSA 2025: **s.109** (may make or adopt regulatory decisions based on another authority's reports/decisions; may enter mutual recognition agreements), **s.110** (laboratory reliance), **s.111** (regulatory harmonisation initiatives). Definitions of "reliance" and "recognition" in s.2.

### 3.5 Unregistered / named-patient / emergency supply

Under **S.I. 163 of 2019** (Botswana's "Section 21" analogue), read directly from the gazette:

- **Reg. 6 — Exemption from registration for individual patient.** *"A medical practitioner may apply in **Form 4**, upon payment of an application fee... to exempt the registration of medicines from outside Botswana, for his or her patient's personal use."* Must comply with the guidelines and **be signed by an importing pharmacist residing in Botswana**. **Validity: six months.**
- **Reg. 7 — Import of unregistered medicines for personal use.** Not more than **one month's** supply; 1–3 months requires a certified copy of the prescription on request; subsequent supplies require a reg. 6 exemption.
- **Reg. 8 — Exemption from registration for wholesale.** *"An applicant may apply to the Authority to exempt the registration of medicines for wholesale from outside Botswana **under special circumstances as determined by the Authority**."* **Form 5** + fee. *"The applicant **may be required to pay for the inspection of the manufacturing site** prior to authorisation."* — **this is the commercially relevant unregistered-supply route for a company.**
- **Reg. 9 — Exemption of donated unregistered medicines** — Form 6.

Processing: **48 working hours**. Carried forward in MRSA 2025 **s.35(4)–(7)** and **s.38** (Registration during public health emergency — may deviate from procedures or waive fees).

An **Emergency Assessment Pathway (EAP)** was described on the archived ZAZIBONA page (requires a WHO-declared PHEIC or Botswana-declared PHE with no licensed product, cGMP compliance, commitment to complete development; assessed via WHO EUL + BoMRA guidelines). **UNVERIFIED on the current site.**

## 5. Local responsible person / local agent

**Yes under the 2025 Act and the draft 2026 regulations; not explicitly under the 2019 regulations — and in practice foreign entities currently hold Botswana registrations directly.**

**MRSA 2025, s.36 — "Applicant to have local presence"** (verbatim):
> **36. (1) An application for a regulated product shall be made by a company registered, licensed or operating in Botswana.**
> **(2) Where the applicant is not resident in Botswana, the applicant shall appoint a local technical representative or authorised representative who shall be resident or incorporated in Botswana.**
> (3) The appointment shall be in such manner as may be prescribed.
> (4) The local technical representative shall be qualified as may be prescribed.

**Draft Human Medicines Regulations 2026, reg. 7(2)–(3)** (verbatim):
> (2) An applicant who is not resident or incorporated in Botswana shall appoint a local technical representative who — (a) **is a natural person ordinarily resident in Botswana or a company incorporated in Botswana**; (b) is responsible for all communications with the Authority; (c) ensures compliance; (d) receives and processes safety reports and complaints; (e) facilitates inspections; and (f) has authority to act on behalf of the MA holder.
> (3) The appointment shall be **in writing, signed by both parties, filed with the Authority before or with the application, and updated within fourteen days of any change.**

**Under the currently in-force instruments** the requirement is weaker: S.I. 163/2019 reg. 3(6) says only *"A marketing authorisation holder shall be responsible for the importation, advertising and promotion of his or her medicine."* The Registration Quality Guideline requires a **letter of authorisation for the responsible person** (Module 1.2.2.2) and proof of registration of a Responsible Pharmacist **only for local manufacturers** (Module 1.7.4).

**Empirical check against the live register** (5 600 registered/compliant records): a `localtechnical_representative` field is populated on **3 037 of 5 600 (54%)**. Registrant countries: **South Africa 3 360, India 963, Botswana 326, Canada 204, Namibia 129, Zimbabwe 63, Germany 36.** Most-used local technical representatives: Apotex Inc (204), Pharma Vision (Pty) Ltd (204), Medswana (Pty) Ltd (184), Novartis (157), Cipla Medpro (128). **So today a foreign company can and does hold the registration itself.** Under the 2025 Act that changes.

## 8. GMP

**Standard applied: WHO GMP.** The GMP guideline cites **WHO Technical Report Series annexes throughout** (TRS 1010 Annex 10 stability, TRS 1010 Annexes 1–2 herbals, TRS 1044/1010 Annex 5 medicinal gases, TRS 902 Annex 9 packaging, TRS 1025 Annex 7 storage & distribution, TRS 1060 Annex 4 blood establishments) and states manufacturers may use any reference texts "that help them attain the **WHO cGMP** Compliance". **It does not adopt the EU GMP Guide or the PIC/S GMP Guide as its standard.**

**Certificates accepted / desk review vs. physical inspection** — from BOMRA/IL/IL/P08/G02 Iss 2.0:

§6.2.1 — BoMRA may rely on GMP inspections by recognised authorities for the same site/block/production line/dosage form, *"This reliance is **unilateral and is implemented through desk assessment** of inspection reports issued by:"*
- **(a) WHO-Listed Authorities as published and periodically updated by the WHO**
- **(b) Regulatory authorities within the SADC region** — SAHPRA, MCAZ (Zimbabwe), ZAMRA (Zambia), TMDA (Tanzania) — including outcomes from **WHO Prequalification** and **ZAZIBONA**
- **(c) Other regulatory authorities e.g. Ghana FDA**

§6.3.1 — **Recognition** (unilateral) of GMP outcomes from **WHO Prequalification**, **ZAZIBONA** and **AMA**. §6.3.2 — facilities approved through these *"may not routinely require an onsite inspection"*.

§6.2.2 — desk assessment requires **complete, accurate and unredacted**: inspection reports; reviewed CAPA plans + evidence of implementation; valid GMP certificates; **APQR reports**; relevant batch manufacturing and packaging records.

Duration/limits:
- §6.2.4 WLA-jurisdiction sites: continued desk reliance if a valid GMP certificate is maintained, routine WLA inspections *"generally not exceeding three (3) to four (4) years"*, no evidence of non-compliance.
- §6.2.5 SADC/other-authority sites: inspection and approval **not older than two (2) years**.
- §6.2.7 GMP clearance by desk assessment valid for **the referenced certificate's validity or two (2) years, whichever is shorter**.
- ⚠️ **§6.2.8 — "For manufacturing facilities located outside WLA jurisdictions, reliance shall be limited to a maximum of two (2) consecutive cycles. Thereafter, an on-site inspection shall be conducted."** **Budget for a physical inspection of an Indian/Chinese CMO by roughly the third cycle.**
- §6.2.3 BoMRA reserves the right to require on-site or remote (virtual) inspection based on risk.
- §6.2.10 Reliance **refused** where inspection scope is irrelevant, unresolved critical/major deficiencies, documentation incomplete/unreliable/**redacted**, data-integrity concerns, or evidence of significant regulatory action/recalls.
- §6.1.4(b) In assessing a reference authority BoMRA considers *"Preferably, WHO GBT Maturity Level 3 and above"*.

**Dossier GMP evidence** (Quality Guideline Module 1.7): list of manufacturers', packers' and Finished Product Release Controls' names and licence numbers with **dates of inspection by the Health Authorities of ICH, PIC/S and SADC**; **inspection reports not older than three years**; **latest GMP certificate not older than three years**.

**PIC/S status — VERIFIED NEGATIVE.** `https://picscheme.org/en/members` fetched. **Botswana/BoMRA is not a PIC/S participating authority, applicant or pre-accession candidate.** The only African PIC/S member is South Africa's SAHPRA (acceded July 2007). Consistent with this, the GMP reliance guideline's revision log records *"Removal of recognition on Regulatory authorities that participate in PIC/S. This was part of the SRA definition."* — **PIC/S membership per se is no longer an independent basis for GMP recognition at BoMRA; WLA status is.**

**GMP fees:** desk review **USD 3 500**; on-site USD 3 500 (SADC) / 5 000 (rest of Africa) / 6 500 (Asia) / 7 000 (rest of world).

## 11. Gotchas — ranked by how much they can hurt

1. ⚠️ **BoMRA has a declared human-medicines assessment BACKLOG.** Stakeholder Notice, **8 July 2026** — target clearance: **New applications → January 2027**; variations filed 2022 → Aug 2026, 2023 → Oct 2026, 2024–25 → Jan 2027; renewals → Jan 2027. BoMRA explicitly asks applicants to *"Utilise reliance and recognition pathways where applicable"* and to share existing approvals from *"WHO Listed Authorities (WLAs), Tanzania, Zambia, South Africa, Zimbabwe and Namibia"*. **Treat the published 18-month standard timeline as aspirational and the 3-month reliance timeline as the only realistic route near-term.** `https://www.bomra.co.bw/stakeholder-notice-implementation-of-the-human-medicines-assessment-backlog-clearance-plan/` — VERIFIED (200)
2. ⚠️ **Legal-basis uncertainty.** Confirm in writing which Act is in force (2013 No. 8 vs 2025 No. 29) and whether any 2026 SI has been gazetted. Fees, the local-representative rule and the pathway taxonomy all differ.
3. ⚠️ **Fee regime change is coming and is expensive for foreign applicants.** Today an orphan registration is **BWP 1 000** with no USD retention fee. The draft moves you to **USD** pricing with **USD 800/year retention** and USD 4 000 five-yearly renewal. **If the orphan route is viable, filing under the current schedule is worth a great deal.**
4. **CPP is mandatory.** Module 1.10.2 — applies to **all applications including multisource/generics**. BoMRA issues its own CPP for BWP 250.
5. **Registration in country of origin is not strictly required — but you must explain.** Module 1.10.1 requires a list of every country where the product has been **submitted, approved, rejected or withdrawn**, with dates and **reasons for rejection or withdrawal**. *"If no application has been submitted for registration in the country of origin, include a statement to provide the reason for this decision."*
6. **Samples.** One sample **in each proposed packaging material**, plus a **CoA whose batch number matches the sample**. Required only after screening is passed.
7. **Language/labelling.** All forms in English. PIL *"written in layman's language (Basic English)"*. Labels *"prepared as per **SADC guideline on product information and labelling**"*. Mock-ups for **all strengths and pack sizes**.
8. **Annual retention is a live cancellation risk and it is public.** Reg. 4: registration valid **five years subject to annual submission of information accompanied by a fee**. The BRIMS public register openly exposes retained vs **unretained** status — currently **5 600 unretained** vs **429 retained**. Competitors and partners can see it.
9. **Renewal deadline is hard.** Reg. 5(2)(c): renewal must be submitted **not later than six months before expiry**.
10. **Query clock.** Max **3 months** to respond; blow it and the application is **rejected/closed**; restarting costs a new fee, new reference number, and an independent decision.
11. **Reliance sameness rule.** Your Botswana dossier must be **identical to the most updated product on record at the reference authority**, with all post-approval variation letters. Divergent local formulations/packs break the pathway.
12. **GMP reliance is capped.** Two consecutive desk-assessment cycles for non-WLA-jurisdiction sites, then a physical inspection (USD 5 000–7 000).
13. **Multi-strength filings must include the BE strength** (G01 §4.1.12).
14. **Ad valorem import fees.** **0.15%** of consignment value ordinary; **0.25%** wholesale-exempted — scales with COGS, unlike the flat permit fee.
15. **MA holder owns importation, advertising and promotion** (reg. 3(6)) — that liability travels with the registration.
16. **BoMRA may publish assessment-report summaries for both approved and rejected applications** (reg. 3(5)). **A rejection can become public.**
17. **Payment reference discipline.** Only the **11-digit BRIMS quotation number** is accepted as a payment reference.
18. **Reg. 8 wholesale exemption may trigger a site inspection at your cost** *before* authorisation — the "quick" unregistered-import route is not always quick.

## 12. Recommended play for an off-patent repurposing portfolio

1. **Query the BRIMS public API first** (open, no auth) to check whether each candidate molecule is already registered in Botswana, by whom, and via which local technical representative — that determines whether you get the draft "generic where the reference medicine is registered in Botswana" abridged route.
2. **Secure a WLA or ZAZIBONA-member approval first** — **South Africa/SAHPRA is the highest-leverage single approval** (both a ZAZIBONA active member and WHO ML3). Then file at BoMRA under reliance for the **3-month** timeline instead of 18.
3. **Apply for orphan designation before or with the application.** Under the current schedule that is **BWP 1 000**; under the draft it is 50% off registration and retention.
4. **Line up a Botswana-incorporated local technical representative now** (use the Licensed Facilities XLSX); already normal practice and becomes mandatory under MRSA 2025 s.36.
5. **Write to registration@bomra.co.bw** to confirm in one message: (a) which Act/SI is currently in force; (b) the exact **screening fee** (the gazette misprint); (c) ZAZIBONA submission windows and reference-country assignment; (d) whether eCTD is accepted.
