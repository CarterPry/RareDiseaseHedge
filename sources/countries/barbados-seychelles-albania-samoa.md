## 1. Barbados

**Bottom line: Barbados has NO drug registration system today — it is a pure import-permit + facility-licensing regime. That is changing right now: the Barbados Medical Products Act, 2026 passed Parliament in June 2026 and will create a registration/marketing-authorisation authority for the first time.**

**Regulatory authority / responsible unit**
- **Barbados Drug Service (BDS)** — Ministry of Health and Wellness. 6th & 7th Floors, Warrens Towers II, Warrens, St. Michael, Barbados W.I. Tel **1 (246) 535-4300**; Fax 1 (246) 535-4342 / 4320; Email **director@drugservice.gov.bb**. Director (Ag.) listed as Ms. Delores Mascoll. Site: http://drugservice.gov.bb/ (**VERIFIED** — HTTP 200; note the site is **HTTP-only**, HTTPS refuses connections, so tools that force HTTPS will fail).
- Ministry of Health and Wellness: https://www.health.gov.bb/ (**VERIFIED**), Tel +1 (246) 536-3800, info@health.gov.bb. Department page https://www.gov.bb/Departments/drug-service (**VERIFIED**).
- BDS self-describes as "the regulatory and operational arm of the pharmaceutical delivery service for the Government of Barbados" — 104 staff, 14 pharmacies. **Your premise is correct: it is overwhelmingly a public drug-benefit/procurement scheme.** Its stated objectives are supply, cost rationalisation, procurement/distribution, free drugs at point of service for eligible persons (65+, under 16, and chronic-disease beneficiaries), maintaining the BNDF, and narcotics control. A **Drug Inspectorate** sits inside BDS and runs "the inspections and licensing programme for pharmacies (public and private) and drug manufacturing plants in keeping with the requirements of the Health Services Act."

**Registration vs. permit — answered directly**
PAHO's *Barbados Pharmaceutical Country Profile* (**VERIFIED**, https://www.paho.org/sites/default/files/2025-06/pharmaceutical-country-profile-barbados.pdf) states in its MRA functions table: **"Marketing authorisation / registration: No"**; Inspection: Yes; Import control: Yes; Licensing: Yes; Market control: No. Body text: *"In Barbados, legal provisions do not require marketing authorization (registration) for all pharmaceutical products on the market, and mutual recognition mechanisms are not in place."* Also "For product registration [testing]: No." Import: "Legal provisions exist requiring authorization to import medicines and importation of medicines through authorized ports of entry." Licensing: importers **Yes**; wholesalers **No**; distributors **No**. There is no quality-control lab in Barbados (testing contracted to the Caribbean Regional Drug Testing Laboratory and Experchem, Canada). PAHO also notes "Barbados does not have a website for the MRA."

**Governing legislation**
- **Drug Service Act, 1980** (creates BDS and the Drug Formulary Committee)
- **Health Services Act, Cap. 44, 1969** (basis for the inspection/licensing programme)
- **Pharmacy Act, 1986** (s.10 creates the Pharmacy Council — registers *persons* and *premises*, not products)
- **Therapeutic Substances Act, 1950** (s.4 makes the Chief Medical Officer the Licensing Authority for manufacture for sale/supply)
- **NEW: Barbados Medical Products Act, 2026** — passed the House and Senate in **June 2026** (Senate passage reported 10 June 2026 by CBC). Section 6 governs registration and marketing authorisation of medical products; s.53 covers pharmacovigilance. Introduced by Senator **Jerome Walcott**. I could **not** locate the official published text of the Act — treat section numbers as press-reported, **UNVERIFIED against the gazette**.

**Recognition / reliance pathway**
- Today: **none in force** (PAHO: "mutual recognition mechanisms are not in place").
- Coming: PAHO stated on **30 July 2026** that the BMPA framework "will incorporate reliance and recognition approaches, allowing the small regulatory system to leverage assessments from established authorities," and that Barbados targets **WHO Maturity Level 3 by 2029**. Source **VERIFIED**: https://www.paho.org/en/news/30-7-2026-barbados-advances-toward-launch-its-medical-products-authority-paho-support (see also https://www.paho.org/en/news/1-7-2026-barbados-approves-creation-barbados-medical-products-authority-support-paho).
- **CARPHA / Caribbean Regulatory System (CRS) matters a great deal here** — it is the de facto reliance route into Barbados. CRS is hosted at CARPHA in Trinidad & Tobago, endorsed by CARICOM COHSOD (2014–2017). Its guidance document is literally titled *"Requirements for the Preparation of a Dossier for Medicines Recommendation for Marketing Authorization/**Import Permit** in CARICOM States"* (**VERIFIED**: https://carpha.org/Portals/0/Documents/CRS_ReqsDS003.pdf). Verified mechanics: CRS does an abbreviated review; on a favourable outcome a recommendation + assessment report goes to all CARICOM governments via appointed focal points and the product is published on a public webpage; **governments are asked to make a marketing authorisation/import permit decision within 60 calendar days**. The sponsor then satisfies three local requirements: **(1) payment of a nominal local user fee, (2) completion of local administrative forms, (3) identification of an importer.**
- **CRS eligibility (verified)**: product must be on the current WHO EML (*non-EML products can be considered with a justified public health need — contact CRSregistration@carpha.org*), **and** hold an MA from one of the **11 PAHO-designated reference authorities: Argentina, Brazil, Canada, Chile, Colombia, Cuba, European Union, Mexico, United Kingdom, United States** — **or** be **WHO-prequalified**. Note: **PMDA/Japan is not on this list.**
- CRS contact: **CRSregistration@carpha.org**. Landing page https://carpha.org/What-We-Do/CRS/Caribbean-Regulatory-System-1 (**VERIFIED**). Public list of CRS-recommended medicines (XLSX, 96 KB): https://carpha.org/Portals/0/Documents/CARPHA%20CRS%20Recommended%20Medicines%20List.xlsx (**VERIFIED**, HTTP 200). CRS passed its 100th recommended medicine.

**How a product actually gets onto the BNDF (this is the real Barbados pathway)**
The **Drug Formulary Committee (DFC)** is "a committee established under the **Drug Service Act** for the purpose of advising the **Minister of Health** on the selection of medicines" (**VERIFIED** at http://drugservice.gov.bb/index.php?id=774). The DFC applies **WHO selection criteria**: must meet the needs of the majority of the population; scientific effectiveness data required; indisputable risk:benefit; acceptable quality; single agents preferred over combinations; listed by **INN**. Where products are therapeutically equivalent, selection is by **cost, scientific research, pharmacokinetic profile, patient compliance, and reliability of the local supplier**. Supply is then via **Maximum Price Contract (MPC)** tenders.
- **Critical commercial gate:** supplier applicants must have **a representative on the island who is a Drug Service-approved local agent**; the local distributor receives product, provides certificates of analysis, and acts as intermediary between government and supplier. At least a **four-month supply** must be available on day one of the contract period, with continuous supply thereafter.
- **Filing contact for formulary submissions:** *The Chairman, Drug Formulary Committee, c/o Barbados Drug Service, 6th Floor, Warrens Tower II, Warrens, St. Michael* (**VERIFIED** from the BNDF preface).

**Public register / formulary — VERIFIED**
- **Barbados National Drug Formulary (BNDF)**, web-based, 2022 edition: https://formulary.drugservice.gov.bb/ (**VERIFIED**, HTTP 200). Preface: https://formulary.drugservice.gov.bb/bndf2022/preface_dashboard.php (**VERIFIED**). Searchable by active ingredient, brand name, therapeutic description, **accepted drugs on contract**, and formulary category (A/B/C); also has deletions & additions, **manufacturer/agent directories**, and cost data. First launched 1980. Note the site serves content from a `challengebb.com/bndf2022/` backend. A specific `manufacturers_list.php` path I tried returned **404 — UNVERIFIED**; navigate from the dashboard instead.
- **There is no register of *authorised products* — because there is no authorisation.** The BNDF is a reimbursement/procurement formulary, not a marketing-authorisation register.

**Timelines and fees — HONEST ANSWER: I could not verify any.** I found **no published fee schedule** for Barbados import permits, importer licences, formulary submission, or (obviously) BMPA registration, and **no published statutory timelines**. The BMPA fee schedule almost certainly does not exist yet. CRS's "nominal local user fee" amount is likewise unpublished. Do not assume any number.

**Recommended filing pathway**
Two-track: (a) run the product through **CARPHA/CRS** (CRSregistration@carpha.org) to obtain a recommendation usable for the Barbados import-permit decision — but check the WHO EML eligibility gate first for a rare-disease repurposed generic, and use the "justified public health need" carve-out; (b) simultaneously appoint a **BDS-approved local agent** and approach the **Chairman, Drug Formulary Committee** for BNDF listing, and **director@drugservice.gov.bb** to confirm current import-permit mechanics and the BMPA transition timetable. Given the Act passed ~7 weeks ago, ask BDS/MoH directly which regime applies to a filing made now.

## 2. Seychelles

**Bottom line: NO medicines registration. Seychelles operates a consignment-by-consignment import permit only, with no product dossier review. There is no medicines register and no marketing authorisation.**

**Regulatory authority / responsible unit**
- **Medicines Regulatory Unit (MRU)**, within the **Regulation & Registration Section**, Ministry of Health. https://www.health.gov.sc/regulation-registration-section/ (**VERIFIED**). The Section comprises the Medicines Regulatory Unit, Health Practices Regulatory Unit, Public Health Boards, and Health Research Ethics Committee.
- **MRU direct contacts (VERIFIED, from the official 2025 import guide): Tel +248 4385035 and +248 4388075; Email MRU@health.gov.sc**
- Ministry of Health general: P.O. Box 52, Victoria, Seychelles Hospital; Tel +248 4388000; customerservice@health.gov.sc. https://www.health.gov.sc/ (**VERIFIED**).
- **Import and Export Permit Unit, Trade Department, Ministry of Finance, National Planning and Trade** — 2nd Floor, Maison Esplanade, Rue De Possession, Victoria. **Tel +248 4382143, 4382162, 4382161; Email iepermit.info@finance.gov.sc** (**VERIFIED**).
- **Pharmaceutical Services** sits under the **Health Care Agency** (procurement arm): https://www.health.gov.sc/health-care-agency/pharmaceutical-services/ (**VERIFIED**). Five units: Hospital & Community Pharmacy Services, **Medicine Procurement Unit**, Drug Quality Control Laboratory (~726 analyses/yr), Pharmaceutical Production Unit (local IV fluids, drops, suspensions, topicals). ~450,000 prescriptions/yr across 17 primary-care and 3 hospital pharmacies, **>500 different medicines in circulation**.
- The Ministry of Health is structured as Parent Ministry + **Health Care Agency** + **Public Health Authority** + National AIDS Council. The Public Health Authority handles disease prevention/control under the Public Health Act — **it is not the medicines regulator**.

**Registration vs. permit — answered directly**
No registration. The **Seychelles National Medicine Policy (Final Draft, 15 May 2009)** states plainly: *"In the absence of a proper medicines regulatory authority… [the regulatory unit endorses] imports and licensing of pharmaceutical premises, even though the final approvals are granted by the Ministry of Finance and the Licensing authority respectively. **There is no registration of medicines and inspection activities are limited.**"* And: *"There are no established quality assurance procedures for pharmaceuticals imported into or manufactured in the country. There is no medicine inspectorate, registration of [medicines]…"* (**VERIFIED**: https://healthresearchwebafrica.org.za/files/SEYCHELLES%20NATIONAL%20MEDICINE%20POLICY.pdf). That policy's own action items 8.2.1/8.3.1/8.4.2 call for *establishing* a medicine regulatory authority, medicines registration, and an import-licence regime tied to registered products — i.e. it was aspirational in 2009.
- **Important caveat on currency:** that document is from 2009. However, the **February 2025** official import guide (below) still describes a pure permit regime with **no product registration step**, which strongly indicates the position is unchanged. I found **no evidence** that a registration system has since been enacted. **I could not verify** the current status of the long-promised Pharmacy Act revision — my search budget was exhausted before I could confirm. Confirm with MRU@health.gov.sc.
- **Trap to avoid:** several AI-generated aggregator pages (notably `everyone.org`) assert a "**Seychelles Medicine Regulatory Authority (SMRA)**". **I found no such body on any official Seychelles government source.** Treat "SMRA" as **UNVERIFIED / likely fabricated**. The real unit is the **MRU**.

**Import permit mechanics (VERIFIED — *Pharmaceutical Import Permit Guide*, Ministry of Finance/Trade Department, Feb 2025)**
https://www.tradeportal.sc/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Pharmaceutical-Import-1.pdf (**VERIFIED**, HTTP 200, 9.1 MB)
- "**The Ministry of Health regulates the Medicine Importation Policy and must approve all applications for pharmaceuticals imports.**"
- Applications go to the **Import and Export Permit Section of the Trade Division** (one-stop shop); permit obtainable electronically. **Permit must be obtained before import.**
- **Permit validity: six (6) months, renewable only once.**
- **Medicines can only be imported per consignment**; human and animal medicines need **separate permits**.
- All consignments must enter through designated ports and are cleared by Customs **in consultation with the inspectorate of the Medicines Regulatory Unit**.
- Commercial importers need a **valid business registration certificate** + **valid business licence** of the appropriate category (Pharmacy / General Sales / Manufacture); proforma invoice must state **generic name, composition, strength, pack sizes, manufacturer's details**. Products in the "Scheduling of Medicines for Health Care Shops" annex may only be imported by **licensed pharmacies**.
- Legal basis: **Customs Management (Prohibited & Restricted Goods) Regulations 2023** (the WTO import-licensing notification cites the 2019 predecessor, S.I. No. 41 of 2019, as amended — S.I. 64 of 2024 also exists).

**Governing legislation**
- **Pharmacy Act, Cap. 169** — originally **1899**, revised **1991**; consolidated text to 30 June 2012 at https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/sey196362.pdf (**VERIFIED**, HTTP 200). It regulates the **practice of pharmacy** and premises — not product approval.
- **Controlled Drugs Act 2016**; **Public Health Act 2015**; **Health Professionals Act 2006**; Customs Management (Prohibited & Restricted Goods) Regulations 2023.

**Reliance / recognition pathway**
**None formalised.** There is no registration to grant, so there is no recognition procedure. In practice the MRU's approval of an import application is discretionary and, per the WTO notification, is "**Non-Automatic licensing to protect human life and health**," with MoH approval used "to verify product safety and source reliability" — i.e. **de facto reliance on the exporting country's approval status via the CPP/source, but no codified reference-regulator list.**

**Timeline and fees**
- WTO Import Licensing portal (**VERIFIED**: https://importlicensing.wto.org/content/pharmaceutical-and-veterinary-products) states for Seychelles: "**There is no import licence fee**," no deposit/advance payment; applications submitted prior to landing; licences "can be granted immediately on request, subject to submission of all relevant documents." **Caveat: this is a WTO notification, not a live Seychelles government fee schedule** — the 2025 Trade Department guide contains **no fee table at all**. Treat "free" as plausible but confirm with iepermit.info@finance.gov.sc.
- The WTO entry also notes restricted-list licences granted "for an open period of one year" but that "**medicinal products require licences per each shipment**" — this partially conflicts with the 2025 guide's 6-month permit. Flagging the discrepancy rather than resolving it.

**Public register / formulary — VERIFIED**
- **No public register of authorised medicines exists** (there are no authorisations).
- **Seychelles List of Essential Medicines 2024** — Ministry of Health / Health Care Agency, adopted/adapted from the WHO Model EML 2023: https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/essential-medicines/national-essential-medicines-lists-(neml)/afro_neml/seychelles-neml-2024.pdf?sfvrsn=72a7371b_1 (**VERIFIED**, HTTP 200). Landing page: https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/seychelles--list-of-essential-medicines-2022-(english).
- AUDA-NEPAD AMRH country page for Seychelles (https://amrh.nepad.org/amrh-countries/seychelles) returned **HTTP 403 — UNVERIFIED**.

**Local agent / importer requirements**
No "marketing authorisation holder representative" concept. What is required is a **Seychelles-licensed importer**: a local entity with a business registration certificate and a business licence of the right category (Pharmacy, General Sales, Health Care Store, Clinic/Hospital, or Manufacture). Practically, you must sell **to** a licensed local pharmacy/wholesaler who applies for the permit.

**Actual filing pathway**
Because Seychelles is a ~100k-population high-income island with public procurement dominated by the Health Care Agency's **Medicine Procurement Unit**, the realistic route is **not regulatory but commercial**: (1) get the molecule onto the **NEML** (Health Care Agency), (2) appoint/partner with a licensed local importer, (3) that importer applies per-consignment via the Trade Division one-stop shop with MRU approval. Start with **MRU@health.gov.sc** (+248 4385035 / 4388075) for the medicines side and **iepermit.info@finance.gov.sc** for the permit mechanics. Historic scale reference: the 2009 policy put public-sector medicine imports at **US$2.7 million (2007)** — this is a very small market, and procurement was done by **direct negotiation** with suppliers, 2–3 times per year, constrained by foreign-exchange availability.

## 3. Albania

**Bottom line: full registration (marketing authorisation) IS required — and Albania has one of the most explicitly reliance-friendly statutes of the four. There is a codified 30-working-day accelerated route for EMA- or US FDA-authorised products, and a conditional-authorisation route that names orphan medicines explicitly.**

**Regulatory authority**
- **Agjencia Kombëtare e Barnave dhe Pajisjeve Mjekësore (AKBPM)** / National Agency for Medicines and Medical Devices. https://akbpm.gov.al/ (**VERIFIED**).
- Address: **Rr. e Dibrës nr. 359/1, Tiranë, Shqipëri**. Email **akbpm@shendetesia.gov.al**. Tel **+355 42 376045**. Hours Mon–Thu 08:00–16:30, Fri 08:00–14:00. Contact page https://akbpm.gov.al/kontakt/ (**VERIFIED**); information request form https://akbpm.gov.al/kontakt/kerkese-per-informacion/.
- Note: some sources list the older address "Rruga e Kavajës, No. 1001" and the ministry email info@shendetesia.gov.al — the **current** agency page gives Rr. e Dibrës 359/1 and akbpm@shendetesia.gov.al. Parent ministry page: https://shendetesia.gov.al/agjencia-kombetare-e-barnave-dhe-pajisjeve-mjekesore-akbpm/.
- Law 105/2014 Art. 17: "The Agency is a specialised institution for the analysis, **registration**, control of medicines and medical devices, inspection of pharmaceutical activities and pharmacovigilance." Art. 17.3 creates the **Komisioni i Përhershëm i Barnave (KPB)** — Permanent Commission on Medicines — an advisory organ to the Minister.
- **Critical structural point: AKBPM does not grant the MA itself. AKBPM assesses and *proposes*; the Minister of Health and Social Protection grants the authorisation by ministerial order (urdhër).** This is visible in the register, where every entry carries an "URDHRI I MINISTRIT/DATA" column (e.g. "AUTORIZIM TREGTIMI 330/12.07.2021").

**Governing legislation (all VERIFIED)**
- **Law No. 105/2014 "Për barnat dhe shërbimin farmaceutik"** (On Medicines and the Pharmaceutical Service), as amended by **Law No. 109/2015 (15.10.2015)**, **Normative Act No. 5 (12.2.2021)**, and **Law No. 95/2022**. Consolidated text: http://akbpm.gov.al/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/ligj-2014-07-31-105_perditesuar-13.pdf (**VERIFIED**, HTTP 200).
- **VKM No. 299, dated 8.4.2015** — Regulation on granting marketing authorisation for medicines and their classification; amended by **VKM 790 (22.09.2015)**, **VKM 432 (8.06.2016)**, **VKM 579 (9.10.2018)**. Consolidated: http://akbpm.gov.al/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/vendim_299_842015.pdf (**VERIFIED**, HTTP 200).
- Related: Law 89/2014 (medical devices); Law 7975 (narcotics/psychotropics); VKM 514/2023 (GMP); Regulation 660 (good storage & distribution). Legislation index: https://akbpm.gov.al/legjislacioni/ (**VERIFIED**).

**Recognition / reliance pathway — the key finding, all VERIFIED from the statute text**
- **Law 105/2014, Art. 12.2** — AKBPM proposes MA to the Minister for: (a) domestically produced medicines; **(b) medicines that hold an MA and circulate in one of: EU Member States, United States of America, Canada, Turkey, Switzerland, Israel, Japan, Australia, United Kingdom**; (c) medicines manufactured in Balkan countries, only where they hold an MA and circulate in their own country; **(ç) medicines authorised by EMA or the US FDA**.
  - **Note for your use case: Japan/PMDA IS on the Albanian list (unlike CARPHA/CRS). WHO-PQ is NOT named in Art. 12.2.**
- **Law 105/2014, Art. 14 — Accelerated procedure:** medicines holding an MA from **EMA or US FDA** "are granted a marketing authorisation by the Agency **within 30 working days** after the date of verification of the authenticity of the submitted documentation."
- **Law 105/2014, Art. 14/1 — Conditional marketing authorisation** (added by Normative Act 5/2021): issued during epidemics/natural disasters only after EMA or FDA conditional MA; **also available for** (a) medicines treating/preventing/diagnosing seriously debilitating or life-threatening diseases, (b) medicines used in emergencies responding to WHO-recognised public-health threats, and **(c) medicines designated as orphan medicines ("barna jetime")**. Valid **1 year**, issued **within 20 working days** of application (5 working days for anti-COVID-19 vaccines). "Bar jetim" is defined at para. 19/1 of the definitions.
- **Law 105/2014, Art. 11.2 — named-patient / unmet-need import:** where a medicine has no similar alternative holding an AKBPM authorisation, **the Minister of Health may authorise importation of medicines that have no AKBPM marketing authorisation**, under rules set by Council of Ministers decision. This is a genuine, statutory rare-disease access route that bypasses registration entirely.

**Timelines (VERIFIED from VKM 299/2015 consolidated text)**
- *Standard procedure:* Art. 4.1 — Agency validates and does preliminary assessment **within 30 days**. Art. 5.2 — Agency evaluates the dossier **within 210 days** from procedure start. Applicant responses to deficiencies: **60 days, extendable to 120** on written justified request. Art. 5.3 — **KPB reviews within 30 days**. Art. 5.4–5.5 — Agency proposes to the Minister; **Minister grants by order**. Art. 5.6 — fee payable **no later than 40 days** from the ministerial order; Art. 5.7 — non-payment within **45 days** triggers a revocation proposal. Art. 5.8 — Agency issues the MA certificate **within 30 days** of bank confirmation of fee payment; **valid 5 years from the date of the ministerial order**, renewable in 5-year periods (Law Art. 11.3–11.4).
- *Accelerated procedure (VKM 299 Art. 7, Chapter III):* applies to products centrally authorised by **EMA** or authorised by **FDA**. Agency validates **within 15 days**; **evaluates within 20 working days**; **KPB reviews within 5 working days**; then ministerial order; fee payable **within 30 days** of the order. Required documents: **WHO-format CPP** (original) showing the product is authorised and circulating in the country of origin via EMA/FDA; **GMP certificate published on EudraGMDP** (http://eudragmdp.ema.europa.eu) valid at application, or a notarised GMP/CPP copy; and the **EMA or FDA marketing authorisation certificate/decision plus the latest amendment**, with English translation.
  - ⚠️ There is a mild inconsistency between Law Art. 14 ("within 30 working days") and VKM 299 Art. 7's 15 days + 20 working days + 5 working days. Present both; don't reconcile them for the client.
- ⚠️ **I want to flag a fabrication risk I caught:** an automated read of VKM 299 initially produced a clean table claiming "Full 210 days / Abridged 105 days / Mutual Recognition 90 days." **The 105-day and 90-day figures do not appear in the actual text.** Only 210 days (standard) and the Art. 7 accelerated timings are real. Discard any 105/90 figure you see elsewhere unless sourced.

**Dossier and local representative requirements (VERIFIED, VKM 299)**
- Full **CTD** (Modules 1–5), electronic + specified hard copy. **Dossier may be submitted in Albanian OR English** (Art. 2.6).
- Separate application **per pharmaceutical form and strength** (Art. 3.3).
- Written application **plus the bank payment mandate proving the application fee was pre-paid**; fee revenues go to the state budget (Art. 3.1). **Fee is non-refundable if the application is rejected at validation** (Art. 4.2.b).
- **Local representative: a foreign MAH is expressly permitted.** Art. 3.7 requires that "the responsible person, representing a company, **including a foreign one**, must submit a written declaration of the marketing authorisation holder **that is not established in the Republic of Albania**." Law 105/2014 definition 59: "Representative of the marketing authorisation holder" is **any legal person, domestic or foreign**, having the right to represent the MAH in Albania. The **responsible person must hold a university degree in medicine, pharmacy or dentistry** (Art. 3.4.dh).
- **CPP** per the WHO-recommended model, issued by the competent authority of the **reference country**, valid at application and issued **no more than 12 months** before submission; must be an original/official document.
- **GMP** via EudraGMDP; one notarised copy per finished-product manufacturing site covers all products from that site for the GMP validity period.
- Every medicine placed on the market must carry the **medicines control stamp (pulla e kontrollit)** issued by AKBPM, and there is a statutory **track-and-trace** system (Law Art. 20). A separate **"autorizim përdorimi" (use authorisation)** is needed to actually place stock on the market (Law Art. 19).

**Actual filing pathway**

## 4. Samoa

**Bottom line: Samoa has no modern drug registration authority — but it is NOT a pure permit regime either. Section 14 of the Drugs Act 1967 contains a real, legally binding "new drug" approval requirement administered by the Ministry of Health's Chief Executive Officer. It is a notification-plus-standstill mechanism, not a safety/efficacy dossier review. And YES — the arrears claim is VERIFIED, at SAT$12 million.**

**Regulatory authority / responsible unit**
- **Ministry of Health, Samoa.** https://www.health.gov.ws/ (**VERIFIED**). Tel **66660** or **68100**; **Call Free 8006440**; Email **enquiries@health.gov.ws**.
- Relevant units (from https://www.health.gov.ws/departments-divisions/, **VERIFIED**): **Quality Assurance, Registration and Professional Development** (Governance & Corporate); **Pharmacy Services** (Clinical Health Services); **Central Pharmaceutical Warehouse Services** (Governance & Corporate). **No direct divisional contact details are published** — go through the main ministry line.
- Per the National Medicines Policy: "The Ministry of Health is in charge of regulating medicines in Samoa through the **Quality Assurance Division**… in-country regulation activities [are] overseen by the Quality Assurance Division **who reports to the Director General**."
- Note the 2007 restructure: the **National Health Service Act 2006** and **Ministry of Health Act 2006 (amended 2019)** split policy/regulation from service delivery; NHS was later re-merged into MoH. There is **no separate medicines agency**.
- Government ministries directory: https://www.samoagovt.ws/directories/government-ministries/ (**VERIFIED**).

**Registration vs. permit — answered directly, with an important nuance**
- **There is no marketing-authorisation regime, no product register, and no dossier review of quality/safety/efficacy.** The National Medicines Policy is explicit: *"although there is partly a stepwise approach to drug registration in the Samoa Medicines Policy, the core elements of drug regulation (i.e., quality, safety, efficacy, and Information) **are not clearly inculcated in the review for product registration**"*; and among Key Actions: *"**Regulatory Framework for Pharmaceuticals is urgently needed**… Review of the Drugs Act 1967 and related Acts is strategic… **Autonomous regulation of medicines is required**."*
- **BUT — verified from the statute itself — Drugs Act 1967, Part 3, s.14 "New drugs":**
  - **s.14(1): "No person may import or manufacture any new drug unless the new drug is approved under this section."**
  - s.14(2): written application to the **Chief Executive Officer** setting out applicant and manufacturer details; marketed name; quantitative statement of ingredients using non-proprietary names; specimen labels; dosage forms; recommended dose/frequency/route; **indications and claims**; **reports of any tests made to establish safety**; **reports of any tests made to control strength, quality, purity or safety**; intended method of marketing in Samoa; and local manufacturing site if applicable.
  - **s.14(3): a 90-day standstill** — no sale, gift, loan, sample, distribution, or advertising of a new drug until **at least 90 days from the date the application was received**, absent prior written consent of the CEO.
  - s.14(4): CEO may approve with or without conditions; **may decline or revoke only where satisfied the drug may have substantially similar pharmacological effect to an illegal drug under the Narcotics Act 1967**. ← The statutory refusal grounds are extremely narrow; this is a **narcotics-diversion screen, not an efficacy review**.
  - **"New drug" is defined (Interpretation) as a substance/preparation within the definition of "drug" "which has not previously been used in Samoa,"** excluding narcotics under the Narcotics Act 1967 and radioactive substances. ← **For a repurposing company this is the pivotal definition: whether your product counts as a "new drug" turns on prior use in Samoa, not on novelty of molecule or indication.**
  - s.14(5): offence; fine up to **500 penalty units** or imprisonment up to **5 years**.
  - Also relevant: **s.7A** (Minister may by Order prohibit sale of any product), **s.15** (distribution of materially changed drugs postponed — duty on the importer), **s.18** (duty of importer or manufacturer to report untoward effects), **s.28** of the regulation-making power enables **licences to be granted by the Chief Executive Officer**.
  - Source: https://www.ag.gov.ws/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Drugs-Act-1967.pdf (**VERIFIED**, HTTP 200).
- **The Pharmacy Act 2007 registers PHARMACISTS, not medicines.** Verified from its arrangement of provisions: Part 2 Pharmacists Council; **Part 5 "REGISTRATION OF PHARMACISTS"**; Part 6 discipline; Part 7 allied pharmacy professionals; Part 8 offences. **There are no product-registration provisions.** https://www.ag.gov.ws/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Pharmacy-Act-2007.pdf (**VERIFIED**).
  - **Trap to avoid:** aggregator sites (again `everyone.org`) cite a "**Medicines and Pharmacy Act (2007)**" governing importation and product quality/safety/efficacy in Samoa. **No such Act exists** — this is contradicted by the actual Pharmacy Act 2007 text. **UNVERIFIED / false.**

**Governing legislation (the five-Act framework, per the National Medicines Policy)**
**Drugs Act 1967** · **Narcotics Act 1967** · **Ministry of Health Act 2006 (amended 2019)** · **National Health Services Act 2006** · **Poisons Act 1968**. The Pharmacy Act 2007 is added elsewhere in the same policy. All are in the *Samoa Health Legislation Handbook*. The policy itself calls this framework "quite old to support the new developments in the pharmaceutical sector."

**Reliance / recognition pathway**
No codified list. But the National Medicines Policy states the operative reality: *"**The country therefore depends on the reliance mechanisms from Australia and New Zealand for importation of pharmaceuticals**, but in-country regulation activities [are] overseen by the Quality Assurance Division."* Samoa also participates in the **Pacific Medicine Testing Programme** run by Australia's **TGA** laboratories branch — **5 free product tests per year per Pacific Island country**; beyond 5 incurs cost. Samoa is **not** a member of the **WHO Programme for International Drug Monitoring (PIDM)**; there is no functioning ADR system, no minilabs, and no clinical trials conducted in Samoa. Practical implication: **TGA and Medsafe approval are the currency that matters**, informally.

**National formulary / essential medicines list — VERIFIED**
- **Samoa Essential Medicines List 2022** — approximately **170 medicines**. https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/samoa--essential-medicines-list-2022-(english) (**VERIFIED**, HTTP 200).
- Selection is meant to run through a **National Medicines and Therapeutics Committee**, but the policy records that this committee **"does not appear to be currently functional"**; the 2022 EML was reviewed by an **ad hoc committee**, with complaints from excluded experts, and considered **clinical evaluation without economic evaluation**. **There is no current Standard Treatment Guideline (STG).**
- **Samoa National Medicines Policy and Plan of Action FY2023/24–FY2027/28** (2nd edition): https://www.health.gov.ws/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/FINAL-NATIONAL-MEDICINES-POLICY-PLAN-OF-ACTION_MASTER-COPY.pdf (**VERIFIED**, HTTP 200).
- **There is no public register of approved products.** Whether MoH maintains an internal list of s.14-approved "new drugs" is **unverified** — worth asking directly.

**Procurement context**
Government finances **100% of public-sector medicine needs**. Supply runs through a **Central Pharmaceutical Warehouse** using **mSupply**; **framework contracting** exists; **Government Tenders Board** review of medicine orders is a named bottleneck. Local pharmaceutical production is explicitly **not on the agenda**. Substantial donation inflows from **WHO, UNDP, UNICEF, UNFPA**, and bilaterally from **China, India, Australia**. Policy Key Action: *"In the short term, **pooled procurement** with local content, among others, could be leveraged"* — i.e. **Pacific pooled procurement is aspirational for Samoa, not yet operational.** No essential-consumables list and no donation guideline were found to exist during the policy review.

**Local agent / importer requirements**

### Cross-cutting notes

- **Reference-regulator acceptance differs materially across the four.** Albania names **EU/EMA, US FDA, Canada, Turkey, Switzerland, Israel, Japan, Australia, UK** in statute (**PMDA included**). CARPHA/CRS — the practical reliance route for Barbados — names **Argentina, Brazil, Canada, Chile, Colombia, EU, Cuba, Mexico, UK, US, and WHO-PQ** (**PMDA excluded**). Seychelles and Samoa name nobody in law; Seychelles relies informally on the CPP/source country, Samoa informally on **TGA/Medsafe**.
- **WHO-PQ is only explicitly usable in the CARPHA/CRS route.** It is not named in Albanian law and has no formal standing in Seychelles or Samoa.
- **Only Albania gives you a real orphan/rare-disease hook** (Law 105/2014 Art. 14/1(c), plus the Art. 11.2 unmet-need ministerial import). Barbados, Seychelles and Samoa have no orphan provisions; access there is via formulary/EML listing and government procurement, not via a regulatory designation.
- **Every one of the four requires an in-country commercial counterparty** — a BDS-approved local agent (Barbados), a licensed local importer (Seychelles), an authorised representative/responsible person with a medical/pharmacy/dentistry degree (Albania), a local tendering agent in practice (Samoa).
- **Fee reality check: I could not verify a single fee amount in any of the four countries.** That is not an oversight — none of the four publishes a medicines fee schedule online. Every fee number must come from a direct ministry enquiry.
- **Watch out for `everyone.org` country guides.** They surfaced repeatedly in search results for all four countries and produced at least two demonstrably false claims (a "Seychelles Medicine Regulatory Authority" and a Samoan "Medicines and Pharmacy Act 2007"). They read authoritatively and are not.
