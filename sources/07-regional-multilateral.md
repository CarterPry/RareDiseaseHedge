# Regional & multilateral drug-registration shortcuts — research findings (as of 5 Aug 2026)

## 1. ZAZIBONA — SADC collaborative medicines registration procedure

**What it is.** A work-sharing/joint-assessment initiative among SADC national medicines regulatory authorities (NMRAs) for dossier assessment and cGMP inspections. Founded October 2013 by **Za**mbia, **Zi**mbabwe, **Bo**tswana, **Na**mibia (hence the name); absorbed into the SADC Medicines Registration Harmonisation project in 2015 under AUDA-NEPAD's AMRH umbrella. Strategic plan runs 2023–2027.

**Membership (VERIFIED — https://zazibona.com/membership/):** All 16 SADC states participate in one of three tiers.
- **Active (9):** Botswana (BoMRA), DR Congo (ACOREP), Malawi (PMRA), Mozambique (ARM), Namibia (NMRC), South Africa (SAHPRA), Tanzania (TMDA), Zambia (ZAMRA), Zimbabwe (MCAZ). Active status requires demonstrated capacity to do both assessments and GMP inspections.
- **Non-active (5):** Angola, Comoros, Madagascar, Seychelles, Eswatini (listed as "Swaziland").
- **Observers (2):** Lesotho, Mauritius.
- Joining requires an NMRA memorandum "agreement to participate."
- Note MCAZ's page (VERIFIED — https://www.mcaz.co.zw/alternative-submission-pathways) gives a slightly different/older observer breakdown; zazibona.com is the authoritative current list. Some secondary sources say "13" or "14 countries" — treat those as stale.

**How you enter (VERIFIED — https://zazibona.com/dossier-assessments/eligibility-criteria/).** There is no single "ZAZIBONA filing." You must:
1. Submit an **identical CTD dossier to a minimum of two participating countries**, and
2. **Pay each of those countries' national registration fees**, and
3. Flag interest in the ZAZIBONA route (or consent when a regulator spots a duplicate pending application and proposes it).
Since **12 February 2024** the procedure is centralised: after national filing, the application goes through a **central online submission portal** (applicant registration form required). TMDA's page adds that **WHO-prequalified products and products going through the WHO Collaborative Registration Procedure are NOT eligible** for ZAZIBONA (VERIFIED — https://www.tmda.go.tz/pages/the-zazibona-collaorative-medicines-registration-procedure).

**Process (VERIFIED — https://zazibona.com/dossier-assessments/our-process-flow/).** One country acts as rapporteur (primary review of Modules 2–5), a second as co-rapporteur (peer review). Quarterly joint assessment sessions produce a **Consolidated Assessment Report (CAR)**, a **Consolidated List of Questions (CLOQ)** and a **recommendation for approval or rejection**. The CLOQ plus any national questions are sent to you separately by each country you filed in. Recommendations then go to each country's national expert committee.

**Timelines.**
- Official target: **median 9 months to recommendation**, inclusive of applicant response time (VERIFIED — https://zazibona.com/statistics/).
- **GMP inspection:** target 90 days from start of inspection to conclusion after CAPA review (VERIFIED — https://zazibona.com/timelines-statistics/).
- **National registration after a positive recommendation: target ≤90 days**, but explicitly "remains the responsibility of each participating country" (VERIFIED — process-flow page). SAHPRA specifically commits to a registration decision **within 90 working days after screening** once a valid application with a positive ZAZIBONA recommendation is received.
- The frequently-quoted "up to 300 days joint assessment + 90 days national MA" figure comes from academic literature (Frontiers/JPPP), not from an official ZAZIBONA page — treat as UNVERIFIED against primary source.

**Fees.**
- **There is no separate ZAZIBONA dossier-assessment fee.** You pay each participating country's ordinary national registration fee. This is the single biggest cost misunderstanding.
- **GMP inspection fees are published and joint (VERIFIED — https://zazibona.com/inspection-fees/), in USD:** non-sterile plant <3 dosage forms $8,000 (3 days); sterile/special plant $10,000 (3 days); plant with 3 dosage forms $10,500 (3–4 days); >3 dosage forms $12,000 (4 days); CRO $8,000 (3 days).
- Illustrative national fees, Zimbabwe (VERIFIED — MCAZ alternative pathways page, excl. VAT): expedited review NCE $4,500 / generic $4,000 / line extension $3,000, 3-month guarantee; WHO CRP $4,000 with 90-day guarantee ($1,500 top-up to convert a pending application).

**What a "ZAZIBONA recommendation" actually gets you.** A **scientific recommendation only — not a marketing authorisation anywhere.** Every one of the 9 active countries still issues its own national MA. The value is (a) one assessment instead of nine, (b) a recommendation you can use to support applications in participating countries you did *not* originally file in, and (c) a formally recognised reliance basis. SAHPRA's Reliance Guideline v5 (VERIFIED, read in full — https://www.sahpra.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/SAHPGL-BAU-01_v5-Reliance-Guideline.pdf) lists "ZAZIBONA collaborative procedure" among procedures usable for reliance/collaborative review, and warns this is **not** the same as a recognition agreement (SAHPRA is still negotiating those). SAHPRA will only accept a ZAZIBONA-route filing **after** a positive recommendation has issued.

**Guidelines.** Central library at https://zazibona.com/guidelines/ (VERIFIED) — 10 documents, all technical/BE-oriented (BE guidelines list, clinical report structure, orally-inhaled therapeutic equivalence, bioanalytical method validation, BCS biowaiver, in-vivo BE organisations, GCP, equilibrium solubility protocol, general BE requirements, data integrity). **No version numbers or dates are published, and there is no published master procedural guideline or fee schedule on the site** — a real gap. Contacts listed: sakhi.vee@gmail.com, +263-242-736981/5; zazibona@mcaz.co.zw. ZAMRA hosts ZAZIBONA operational templates (cover letter, acceptance letter, expression of interest, assessment process workflow, roles of key players, API/FPP LoQ template) — VERIFIED https://www.zamra.co.zm/guidelines/.

**Is ZAZIBONA being absorbed into AMA?** **Not directly, and not yet.** SADC's own statement (VERIFIED — https://www.sadc.int/latest-news/sadc-helps-increase-availability-quality-assured-medicines-patients-region) makes **no mention** of transition to AMA and points to continued operation through 2027; it reports 44 joint assessment sessions and 350+ products assessed in ten years, plus 150+ NMRA staff trained. Structurally ZAZIBONA sits under AMRH (AUDA-NEPAD), and AMRH formally handed over to AMA in **January 2026** (VERIFIED — EMA AMA page), so the REC-level procedures are expected to become AMA's regional arms over time. But as of Aug 2026 ZAZIBONA runs its own portal, its own quarterly sessions and its own fee schedule. Treat "ZAZIBONA is going away" as speculation.

**Gotchas.** (1) You pay N national fees, not one. (2) WHO-PQ products are excluded — if your product is PQ'd, use WHO CRP instead. (3) The recommendation is advisory; a national committee can still say no. (4) Nothing binds the non-active/observer members. (5) South Africa is the only ZAZIBONA member that is a PIC/S authority.

## 2. African Medicines Agency (AMA), AU Model Law, AMRH and the RECs

**Treaty status — hard numbers.** The AU's own status list, **dated 22 May 2026** (VERIFIED — I fetched and read the PDF: https://au.int/sites/default/files/treaties/36892-sl-TREATY_FOR_THE_ESTABLISHMENT_OF_THE_AFRICAN_MEDICINES_AGENCY_5.pdf; landing page VERIFIED at https://au.int/en/treaties/treaty-establishment-african-medicines-agency):
- **32 signatures, 31 ratifications/accessions, 31 deposits**, out of 55 AU members.
- Adopted 11 Feb 2019 (32nd Ordinary Session, Addis Ababa); **entered into force 5 November 2021** on deposit of the 15th instrument.
- **The 31 that have ratified:** Algeria, Benin, Botswana, Burkina Faso, Cameroon, Cape Verde, Chad, Congo, Côte d'Ivoire, Egypt, Ethiopia, Gabon, Ghana, Guinea, Kenya, Lesotho, Madagascar*(no — see below)*, Mali, Mauritius, Morocco, Namibia, Niger, Rwanda, Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic, Senegal, Seychelles, Sierra Leone, Tanzania, Tunisia, Uganda, Zambia, Zimbabwe. (Corrected: Madagascar signed 01/08/2019 but has **not** ratified; the 31 are the list above minus Madagascar.)
- **Signed but not ratified (7):** Burundi, Comoros, Equatorial Guinea, Madagascar, Mozambique, São Tomé & Príncipe, Togo.
- **Neither signed nor ratified (17) — commercially significant:** **Nigeria, South Africa, DR Congo, Malawi, Angola, Eswatini, Sudan, Libya**, plus Central African Rep., Djibouti, Eritrea, Gambia, Guinea-Bissau, Liberia, Mauritania, Somalia, South Sudan.
- **Read that carefully: the two biggest African pharma markets — Nigeria and South Africa — are not parties.** Secondary sources reporting "31 as of Dec 2025" are consistent with the official list; the au-ama.africa homepage counter displays "0" (placeholder) and is not usable.

**Operational status.** AMA is a **specialised agency of the African Union**, HQ **Kigali, Rwanda** (host-country agreement with the Government of Rwanda; HQ inaugurated November 2024). **Director-General: Dr Delese Mimi Darko**, appointed June 2025 (VERIFIED — https://www.who.int/news/item/21-05-2026-who-and-african-medicines-agency-launch-landmark-framework-to-strengthen-access-to-quality-assured-health-products-in-africa). AMRH formally handed over to AMA in **January 2026** (VERIFIED — https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/partners-networks/international-activities/multilateral-coalitions-initiatives/african-medicines-agency-african-union). On **21 May 2026**, at WHA79, WHO and AMA signed a **Framework Agreement** covering harmonisation and reliance, safety surveillance of SF products, workforce, local production and emergency preparedness, building on the AU–WHO MoU of May 2025 — with joint operational plans over the next 3–5 years. AMA's own site (VERIFIED — https://au-ama.africa/) describes it as "moving from mandate to delivery" but publishes no fee schedule, work programme or procedure documents; several counters show placeholder zeros.

**What AMA actually does (this is the key commercial question).** **Scientific opinion + "continental listing," not a continental marketing authorisation.** The mechanism is the **Continental Procedure for Evaluation and Listing of Human Medicinal Products**, run by the AMRH **Evaluation of Medicinal Products Technical Committee (EMP-TC)** with the **GMP-TC**, with outputs published in the **"Green Book" — the African Registry of Continentally Listed Products**. National regulators still grant the MA; countries must have enabling provisions to rely on AMA outputs, and many do not.

**Pilot results (VERIFIED — EMA AMA page).** Pilot ran **November 2023 – October 2025**, with technical support from EMA and WHO, EMA financing assessment of 10 products, co-funded by the Gates Foundation, plus a €10m EU grant (May 2023). Outcome: **21 human medicinal products assessed — 12 listed, 2 rejected, 7 withdrawn.** A retrospective evaluation (medRxiv preprint 2026.04.22.26351547 — **UNVERIFIED, 403 on fetch**; figures below via search snippet only) reports 64 expressions of interest → 24 to full evaluation → 12 positive continental opinions, 10 within the ≤210 working-day target; national uptake split as full reliance 7 (58%), sequential reliance 3 (25%), supplemented national review 2 (17%). AMRH Steering Committee approved five *additional* products for continental listing at its 17–18 July 2025 session (**UNVERIFIED** — nepad.org and amrh.nepad.org both return 403; Green Book 2nd edition July 2025 could not be retrieved).

**Reliance on AMA is already live at national level.** SAHPRA's Reliance Guideline v5 (Aug 2025, VERIFIED) explicitly added **AMA** and **EU-M4all** to the list of procedures usable for reliance/collaborative review — this was the stated reason for the v5 revision. SAHPRA has since issued a dedicated **"African Medicines Agency (AMA) Continental Listing Procedure"** communication to industry, **doc PEM-PRE01-2025/26 v1, updated 6 Feb 2026** (landing page VERIFIED — https://www.sahpra.org.za/document/african-medicines-agency-ama-continental-listing-procedure/; the procedure PDF itself sits behind a SharePoint link I could not retrieve — **fee/eligibility detail UNVERIFIED**). MCAZ also lists an "AMA pathway" (joint assessments, clinical trial reviews, biosimilars, API facility inspections; mark "AMA" on the application, CTD format) — VERIFIED.

**AU Model Law on Medical Products Regulation.** Endorsed by AU Heads of State in **2016**; aims to harmonise NRA legal frameworks and enable reliance/mutual recognition. AUDA-NEPAD coordinates domestication and published *A Guidance Document for Domestication of the AU Model Law*. A target of ≥25 countries domesticating by 2020 was **missed**. Documented barriers: human/financial resources, competing national priorities, overlapping institutional mandates, slow legislative amendment. (Sources are the AUDA-NEPAD publication page and Frontiers in Medicine 2023 art. 1117439 — I could not fetch nepad.org, so the domestication count is **UNVERIFIED**.)

**AMRH and the RECs.** AMRH is a component of the AU's Pharmaceutical Manufacturing Plan for Africa, continentally led by AUDA-NEPAD (VERIFIED — https://www.tmda.go.tz/pages/african-medicines-regulatory-harmonization-amrh). Operating model: **90-day registration timeline target; CTD dossier filed in at least two participating countries; abridged review available for products approved by stringent authorities where full assessment reports are accessible;** priority therapeutic areas HIV, malaria, TB, reproductive health.
- **EAC** (Burundi, Kenya, Rwanda, South Sudan, Uganda, DRC, Tanzania) — the oldest functioning joint review procedure alongside ZAZIBONA.
- **SADC / ZAZIBONA** — see §1.
- **ECOWAS/WAHO** — AMRH launched February 2015; medicines-registration harmonisation ongoing (WAHO page: https://www.wahooas.org/web-ooas/en/actualites/cote-divoire/harmonization-medicines-registration-ecowas-region — **UNVERIFIED**, not fetched).
- **ECCAS/OCEAC** and **IGAD** — IGAD is at the "preparing a proposal to participate" stage. **North Africa (NA-MRH)** held its inaugural meeting 18–20 Feb 2025 in Egypt. (These last items are from search snippets — **UNVERIFIED**.)

**Gotchas.** (1) AMA gives an opinion, not an MA — the "continental marketing authorisation" framing is wrong today. (2) Nigeria and South Africa aren't treaty parties, though SAHPRA relies on AMA output anyway. (3) Ratification ≠ enabling domestic law; the gap between the 31 ratifiers and the far smaller set with reliance provisions in national law is the practical constraint. (4) There is no published AMA fee schedule or open rolling call — the pilot ran on expressions of interest.

## 3. Eurasian Economic Union (EAEU) common pharmaceutical market

**Members (VERIFIED — https://eec.eaeunion.org/en/comission/department/deptexreg/ls1/default.php):** **Armenia, Belarus, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Russia.** Yes, Armenia is in.

**Legal basis.** Treaty on the EAEU, 29 May 2014; **Agreement on Common Principles and Rules for Circulation of Medicinal Products within the EAEU, 23 December 2014**; **EEC Council Decision No. 78 of 3 November 2016 — "Rules of registration and expert examination of medicines for human use,"** in force 6 May 2017. Documents hub: eec.eaeunion.org (VERIFIED). Regulatory-impact/public-consultation portal: https://regulation.eaeunion.org/.

**The two procedures (VERIFIED — Decision No. 78 text at https://cis-legislation.com/document.fwx?rgn=93271):**
- **Mutual Recognition Procedure (Item 21):** registration is completed **first in the reference state**; recognition states then register on the applicant's request (or their own initiative), and multiple recognition states may run simultaneously.
- **Decentralised Procedure (Item 22):** several member states register **concurrently**; the applicant designates one of them as reference state.
- **Reference state** runs the full cycle (completeness check, substantive expert examination, inspections) and produces the expert assessment report. **Recognition states** review the reference state's report plus specific CTD modules — they do not re-do the assessment.

**Statutory timelines.** Decision No. 78 (VERIFIED, in **working days**): reference-state process **140** total (completeness assessment 10; substantive file examination 105; decision issuance 10), applicant response allowance **90**; recognition-state examination **40**; recognition-state registration **60**. Practitioner sources render these as ~**210 calendar days in the reference state / up to 90–100 calendar days in a recognition state** excluding clock stops (**UNVERIFIED** — commercial RA firms only). A registration certificate is issued to a uniform Union model, **valid 5 years**, and the product is entered in the **Unified Register of Registered Medicines of the EAEU**.

**The deadlines — current state, VERIFIED.**
- **31 Dec 2020:** last date for *new* national-procedure filings; from 1 Jan 2021 the two EAEU procedures became the only routes for new registrations.
- **31 Dec 2025:** end of the transition period for submitting requests to bring existing national dossiers into EAEU conformity. EEC's Director of Technical Regulation Talgat Momyshev is on record on the Commission's own site that **"this deadline will not change"** — only the conditions in concerned states would be relaxed (VERIFIED — https://eec.eaeunion.org/en/news/komissiya-prodolzhaet-sovershenstvovanie-obshchego-rynka-lekarstv-/). **It was not extended.** (Contrast: the analogous **medical-device** deadline *was* pushed from 31 Dec 2025 to **31 Dec 2027** — do not confuse the two.)
- **Amendments to Decision No. 78 in force 21 June 2025** (VERIFIED — https://eec.eaeunion.org/en/news/uproshcheny-registratsionnye-protsedury-lekarstvennykh-sredstv-na-obshchem-rynke-eaes/) simplified the conformity procedure, expedited manufacturer inspections during authorisation, and **removed the bar on running a mutual-recognition procedure and a variation simultaneously**.
- **Relief mechanics:** if a conformity application was filed with the reference state by 31 Dec 2025 and the procedure is unfinished, the national RC's validity is extended **up to 3 years from the filing date**; where the reference-state procedure completed by 31 Dec 2025, validity in concerned states extends for the duration of the procedure but **≤2 years from that date**. Decision No. 78's transition article also permits pre-1 Jan 2016 national registrations to run under national rules to 31 Dec 2025. Product already imported before the deadline but not conformed may stay in circulation to end of shelf life. **From 1 January 2026, national-legislation registration certificates are cancelled.** (Relief and cancellation details are from RA-firm and Lexology summaries plus the Decision No. 78 text — the conformity/extension arithmetic is **partially UNVERIFIED** against the EEC's own English pages.)
- The **conformity ("bringing into compliance") procedure itself is a simplified ~100 calendar-day route** (**UNVERIFIED** — practitioner sources).

**What this means for an Armenia-targeted filing.**
- Armenia's NRA is the **Scientific Centre of Drug and Medical Technology Expertise (SCDMTE)** under the Ministry of Health. Armenia participates in the unified procedure as reference **or** concerned state. (pharm.am pages **UNVERIFIED** — the host returned a TLS certificate validation error on fetch; treat Armenia-specific fee/form detail as unconfirmed.)
- **Can you file in Armenia and get recognition in Russia? In principle yes** — that is exactly what the mutual-recognition procedure is designed to do: register in Armenia as reference state, then extend to Russia/Belarus/Kazakhstan/Kyrgyzstan as recognition states. In practice this is a *bad* plan: Russia is by far the largest market and, per practitioner analysis, Russian national procedure can run independently; the reference state must have the inspectorate and assessor capacity to carry a full cycle, and Armenia's is small; and each recognition state can still raise its own questions.
- **Sanctions reality.** Medicines are broadly carved out of Western sanctions on humanitarian grounds, but the exemption is applied narrowly and does not cover the distributors, contract manufacturers, banks or logistics around them. Armenia is repeatedly flagged as a **secondary-sanctions and correspondent-banking risk jurisdiction** because of its EAEU membership and geography; Armenian firms with international exposure routinely screen against US/EU/UK lists to protect banking relationships. Russia's own PIC/S membership application is **frozen** (VERIFIED — picscheme.org applicants page). Practical read: **an EAEU registration is a Russia-linked regulatory asset.** For a Western-financed rare-disease company, an Armenia filing that produces a Russian recognition is a compliance, banking and reputational liability that will likely outweigh the market value. (Sanctions material here is from search snippets and legal-practice commentary — **UNVERIFIED** against primary OFAC/EU texts.)

**Gotchas.** (1) There is no Armenia-only path anymore for new products — national registration ended 1 Jan 2021, so any Armenian filing is an EAEU filing. (2) If your product had an Armenian national RC and you missed 31 Dec 2025, it is cancelled as of 1 Jan 2026. (3) Reference-state choice is a strategic, not administrative, decision — it determines who inspects your sites. (4) EAEU GMP certificates issued from 2021 began expiring in 2025; re-inspection requests should be filed 6–9 months ahead.

## 4. Caribbean: CARPHA / Caribbean Regulatory System (CRS) and OECS/PPS

**What the CRS is (VERIFIED — https://carpha.org/What-We-Do/CRS/Caribbean-Regulatory-System and the guidance PDFs below).** A **regional reliance mechanism**, not a regulator. Housed at CARPHA in Port of Spain, Trinidad & Tobago; implemented April 2016 in technical collaboration with PAHO/WHO; originates from the Caribbean Pharmaceutical Policy (2011) and CARICOM COHSOD ministerial endorsements in 2014–2017. Serves CARICOM (an initiative of 15 Member States, ~17m people).

**What it issues — and what happens next (VERIFIED — https://carpha.org/Portals/0/Documents/CRS_ReqsDS003.pdf, read in full).** On a favourable review the CRS issues a **recommendation certificate + assessment report**, disseminated to **all CARICOM governments via government-appointed CRS focal points**, and the product is published on a public list. **Governments are then asked to make a marketing-authorisation/import-permit decision within 60 calendar days.** The applicant must then satisfy **three local requirements in each country**: (1) pay a nominal local user fee, (2) complete any local administrative forms for record-keeping, (3) **identify an importer/distributor**. The CRS will, on request, supply lists of licensed importers operating in CARICOM states. **So: a CRS recommendation is a reliance dossier plus a 60-day political clock, not a marketing authorisation.**

**Review type and timeline (VERIFIED — https://carpha.org/Portals/0/Documents/CRS_Verfication_Review_Guidance_v4_11.20.2020.pdf, GD-ReqDS004, read in full).** The CRS runs a **verification review** — a reliance procedure adapted from WHO PQ, verifying "sameness" against what a reference authority approved. **After payment of the CRS user fee, verification review takes approximately 4–8 weeks** once the dossier satisfies eligibility. Nine documented steps (application → invoice/dossier → screening → queries → assessment → further queries → decision (approve/reject/suspend) → recommendation issued → dissemination) (VERIFIED — https://carpha.org/What-We-Do/CRS/CRS-Review-Process).

**Eligibility (VERIFIED, two documents).**
1. **Essential medicine** — on the current WHO EML (same API and route/dosage form; strength may differ, e.g. 250 mg vs 100 mg). Non-EML products can be considered on justified public-health need, with the CRS consulting CARICOM's **TECHPHARM** committee. **This is the gate that matters for a rare-disease repurposing play** — non-EML products are possible but discretionary.
2. **Approved by a CRS reference authority, or WHO-prequalified.** GD-ReqDS003 (2017) names **11 PAHO-designated RAs**: Argentina (ANMAT), Brazil (ANVISA), Canada, Chile (ISP), Colombia (INVIMA), Cuba (CECMED), EU, Mexico (COFEPRIS), UK, USA, and WHO PQ. The later GD-ReqDS004 Table 1 expands the **extra-regional** tier to: Australia TGA, EMA/EU, UK MHRA, Japan PMDA, Swissmedic, **Iceland, Norway, Liechtenstein** — with regional tier Argentina, Brazil, Canada, Chile, Colombia, Cuba, Mexico, US FDA. Vaccines additionally require WHO PQ **and** applicant consent to CRS use of the **WHO Collaborative Registration Procedure** on CARICOM's behalf. Biotherapeutics/biosimilars have a separate guidance.
3. **Payment of the CRS user fee** before the dossier is accepted; non-refundable; payable by **PayPal to crsregistration@carpha.org or bank wire**. **Different formulations/dosage forms/strengths of the same entity are separate products and separate applications.**

**Fees.** **The fee schedule is not published.** GD-ReqDS004 says only "applicants are advised to contact the CRS for the current schedule of fees." The online application form notes "Application fees shown include a transaction fee of $15.00" but does not display the base amount (VERIFIED — https://carpha.org/What-We-Do/CRS/CRS-Application). **Any specific CRS fee figure you see elsewhere is UNVERIFIED.**

**Submission mechanics (VERIFIED).** Electronic only, to **CRSregistration@carpha.org** or secure file transfer (paper/CDs discouraged); single file ≤50MB or split; bookmarked/hyperlinked navigation strongly recommended; ICH CTD or WHO PQ format accepted. There is also a **CRS Gateway** applicant portal (guide v1.0, 31 July 2023 — https://www.carpha.org/Portals/0/Documents/CRS%20GATEWAY%20Guide%20for%20Applicants%20v1.0_07.2023.pdf; fetched but text not extractable — content **UNVERIFIED**). Post-approval changes: notify the CRS **within 60 days** of submission to the reference authority (GD2.1, 2023).

**Is there a published list of CRS-recommended products? Yes — but it is badly stale.** I downloaded and parsed **https://carpha.org/Portals/0/Documents/CARPHA%20CRS%20Recommended%20Medicines%20List.xlsx** (VERIFIED, parsed programmatically): **286 product rows, highest product number 285, recommendations dated 20 Apr 2017 → 17 Nov 2022 — nothing after November 2022.** Columns: product number, CRS dossier control number, INN/strength/formulation, therapeutic use, issued-to, date of recommendation, valid-until, reference-authority registration number, SmPC/MA link, PIL link, post-recommendation comments. Sheet 2 is a **withdrawals** list (e.g. nevirapine 200 mg and lamivudine 150 mg withdrawn when WHO PQ lapsed — recommendations track the reference approval and die with it). Content skews heavily to HIV/ARVs, vaccines, oncology and transplant immunosuppressants. Meanwhile CARPHA states **353 medical products recommended since 2017, 13 in 2025** (VERIFIED — https://thevoiceslu.com/2025/06/carpha-strengthens-medicine-and-vaccine-oversight-across-the-caribbean-through-regulatory-support-system/). **The live source is the CRS Registry at https://crsregistry.carpha.org/** (homepage VERIFIED — search by trade name, INN, product type, MAH, plus advanced search; deep-link product listing pages returned 404 for me, so the registry's current count is **UNVERIFIED**).

**Which CARICOM states actually act on CRS recommendations.** The honest answer is: **few, and unevenly.** Published uptake (VERIFIED — CARPHA via The Voice, June 2025): **Guyana 164 products** (using CRS since October 2017), **Haiti 5**, **The Bahamas 2**. CARPHA also shared 742 product safety reports with Member States. Executive Director Dr Lisa Indar frames it as faster access "while using fewer national resources." Several Caribbean states have **no legal framework for marketing authorisation at all** and instead issue an **import permit** — the CRS guidance explicitly accommodates this. **Guyana is effectively the only high-volume adopter.**

**Rare-disease angle.** PAHO's PRAIS platform reports the **first CARPHA-CRS recommendation for a rare-disease medicine** (https://prais.paho.org/en/carpha-crs-rare-disease/ — **UNVERIFIED**, host returned a TLS certificate mismatch on fetch). Worth chasing directly with CARPHA, since it would be the precedent for the non-EML/TECHPHARM route.

**OECS Pharmaceutical Procurement Service (PPS) — a separate, pooled-procurement route.** Operating since 1986; ~30+ years; run out of Saint Lucia. **Not a registration mechanism** — it buys. Scope: an **840-item portfolio** (pharmaceuticals, medical supplies, contraceptives, radiological supplies); **~20% reduction in regional medicine costs and ~USD $4m/yr collective savings**; **biennial tender cycle** reviewed by a Joint Tenders Advisory Committee (VERIFIED — https://pressroom.oecs.int/oecs-pharmaceutical-procurement-service-grows-from-strength-to-strength). OECS membership: Antigua & Barbuda, Dominica, Grenada, Montserrat, St Kitts & Nevis, Saint Lucia, St Vincent & the Grenadines, British Virgin Islands, Anguilla, Martinique, Guadeloupe, Saint Martin. Contact: **Francis Burnett, Head of PPS — fburnett@oecs.org, +1 758-455-6361**.
**Getting on the tender list:** prequalification is mandatory and gated — **"OECS/PPS will invite only tenderers that are prequalified and listed in the OECS/PPS suppliers' directory"**; approved suppliers are prequalified via a **vendors registration questionnaire**; products must conform to **WHO GMP, GSP and GDP**; and **prequalified suppliers that skip two consecutive tenders are deleted from the directory.** Bidding runs through the **ePPSS e-procurement platform** (https://procurement.oecs.org/epps/home.do), introduced 2014. (Prequalification-rule specifics come from the OECS prequalification tender document and South-South Galaxy summaries — **UNVERIFIED** as to current edition; the pressroom article is VERIFIED.)

**Gotchas.** (1) CRS gives you a recommendation and a 60-day request, not an MA; you still need a local importer and a local fee in each state. (2) The EML gate is the binding constraint for a rare-disease product — budget for a TECHPHARM public-health-need argument. (3) Your CRS recommendation is derivative: if the reference approval or WHO PQ lapses, the recommendation is withdrawn. (4) Real-world uptake is concentrated in Guyana. (5) OECS/PPS is a completely separate track with its own prequalification — and a use-it-or-lose-it directory rule.

## 5. Pacific Island Countries and Territories (PICTs) — what exists vs what doesn't

**Bottom line: there is no regional registration scheme for the Pacific. None. There is a cooperation forum and there is pooled procurement — those are different things.**

**What exists.**

**(a) WPRA — Western Pacific Regional Alliance of NRAs for Medical Products.** A **voluntary cooperation mechanism convened by WHO WPRO since 2011** to promote cooperation, reliance and effective regulation. It is a network, **not a registration route**; there is no WPRA dossier, fee or recommendation. Its NRA directory (VERIFIED — https://wpralliance.org/nras) lists ~28 countries/territories. Pacific entries **with a named authority**: **Cook Islands** (Ministry of Health), **Fiji** (Fiji Medicines Regulatory Authority), **Kiribati** (Medicines and Therapeutics Committee), **Papua New Guinea** (Pharmaceutical Services Standards Branch), **Samoa** (Ministry of Health), **Vanuatu** (National Drugs and Therapeutic Committee). Listed **without contact details** — i.e. no identifiable functioning authority: **Federated States of Micronesia, Marshall Islands, Nauru, Niue, Palau, Solomon Islands.** Tonga and Tuvalu appear in the Pacific grouping.

**(b) Fiji — the only PICT with a real, transactional registration system.** Legal basis: **Medicinal Products Act 2011, s29** — no medicinal product may be imported, manufactured, exported, stored, sold, distributed, transported, offered for sale or advertised unless registered with the **Fiji Medicinal Products Board**; s30 sets out two registers, the **Provisionally Authorised Medicinal Products Register** and the **Registered Medicinal Products Register** (VERIFIED — https://www.health.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Provisional-Registration-FAQs.pdf, March 2024, read in full). Key operational facts:
- **The Fiji MRA explicitly runs on recognition and reliance** on decisions by recognised overseas NRAs. **Products registered in Australia (ARTG) or New Zealand (Medsafe) get a streamlined path — the online portal pulls their public data and pre-populates the form.** Products registered elsewhere must be entered manually, with **Certificate of Registration/MA + a WHO-format CoPP**; GMP certificate optional-but-helpful (EudraGMDP accepted as a source).
- Applicants must be **registered with the Fiji Competition and Consumer Commission and hold a valid licence to deal in medicinal products in Fiji** — i.e. **you need a local entity/agent.**
- Submissions via the **Fiji MRA Online Services Portal, https://fijimedreg.conforma.systems/login**.
- **Import permits are issued only for listed products, to applicants with an approved listing.** Listings are applicant-specific — the same product can be listed separately by multiple applicants, and one applicant's listing does not cover another's.
- Listing persists until expiry, until an application to the full Registered Medicinal Products Register is approved, or until the Board removes it/ends the provisional register.
- **Fees and statutory timelines are not published** on the Fiji MRA pages or the FAQ — **UNVERIFIED** (contact FijiMRA@health.gov.fj, +679 8921665).
- Separately, **prequalification via Fiji Pharmaceutical & Biomedical Services (FPBS)** is **mandatory** for anything the Ministry of Health procures — local *and* overseas suppliers. Guideline: https://www.health.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/GUIDELINE-FOR-THE-PREQUALIFICATION-OF-PHARMACEUTICAL-PRODUCTS-FOR-THE-FIJI-MINISTRY-OF-HEALTH-MEDICAL-SERVICES-Amended-June-2023.pdf (**UNVERIFIED** — not fetched). FPBS is a *national* procurement hub, **not** a regional one — I found no evidence it procures on behalf of other PICTs.

**(c) Papua New Guinea — a legal framework in reform, thin execution.** **Medicines and Cosmetics Act 1999 + Medicines and Cosmetics Regulation 2002**, administered by the **Pharmaceutical Services Standards Branch (PSSB)** of the National Department of Health, covering registration of medicinal products and licensing of dealers, plus lab testing, inspection and pharmacovigilance. PNG launched a registration system in 2019; a **2024 WHO Global Benchmarking assessment** produced an **Institutional Development Plan**, and a WHO/NDoH workshop on **12 August 2025** reviewed a **draft Medicines Bill** and aligned the CAPA plan with the IDP (WHO PNG news pages — **UNVERIFIED**, not fetched). Read: PNG is mid-reform; do not assume a predictable registration timeline.

**(d) Pooled procurement — this is the real Pacific access route.**
- **UNICEF Vaccine Independence Initiative (VII)**, launched **1991** specifically for Pacific island countries (Cook Islands, Fiji, Kiribati, Marshall Islands, FSM, Nauru, Niue, Samoa, Solomon Islands, Tokelau, Tonga, Tuvalu, Vanuatu), giving them credit facilities and pooled procurement of national vaccine requirements through **UNICEF Supply Division**; **expanded in 2015 from vaccines to all essential commodities.** This is the closest thing to a functioning regional mechanism.
- A **regional pooled procurement mechanism for the Western Pacific has been surveyed but not established** — of 13 countries surveyed, 5 were interested, 5 wanted more information or deferred, **3 were opposed** (PLOS Global Public Health, https://journals.plos.org/globalpublichealth/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgph.0000801 — **UNVERIFIED**, not fetched).
- Global Fund/PSM and UNFPA operate in the region but as vertical-programme procurers, not regional registration bodies. **I found no evidence of a "Pacific Pharmaceutical Procurement" entity analogous to OECS/PPS.**

**(e) Australia's Indo-Pacific Regulatory Strengthening Program (RSP).** DFAT + TGA co-delivered, ~**AUD $11.24m**, launched October 2018 under the Health Security Initiative, extended and now covering **22 countries**; works only with countries that **have** an NRA, on marketing-authorisation systems, reliance and regional collaboration. The TGA is a **WHO Listed Authority**, which is why "approved by TGA" is the single most useful credential in the Pacific. (https://indopacifichealthsecurity.dfat.gov.au/investments/indo-pacific-regulatory-strengthening-program — **UNVERIFIED**, not fetched.)

**What does NOT exist — be blunt about this.** No Pacific equivalent of ZAZIBONA or the CRS. No regional dossier, no regional recommendation, no regional register. WHO's own framing is that Pacific regulatory-system development is "either non-existent or very limited," and that having pharmaceutical legislation on the books ≠ implementation, the binding constraints being human, technical and financial capacity. During COVID, **Tonga and Vanuatu — which do have laws mandating regulation of medical products — in practice relied on wholesalers' supply chains and WHO lab support** rather than on their own regulatory assessment. **Vanuatu and Solomon Islands run fragmented procurement**, producing higher prices and stock-outs. WHO WPRO's own regulatory-landscape report (landing page VERIFIED — https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789290619321, 3 Jan 2024) profiles **only Fiji and Papua New Guinea** among PICTs, alongside 14 larger Asia-Pacific economies — which is itself the answer to "does anyone else have a real authority." (The IRIS full-text PDF returned 403 — **UNVERIFIED**.)

**Practical route for a rare-disease generic.** Fiji registration/listing (leveraging an Australian or NZ approval) is the only genuine registration play. Everywhere else, access runs through **import permits, national essential-medicines lists, ministry tenders, and UNICEF Supply Division** — sell into procurement, don't try to register.

**What PIC/S membership buys you in a reliance filing.** It does **not** create automatic acceptance of a GMP certificate. It creates the mutual confidence that lets a relying authority accept another authority's inspection outcome without re-inspecting. In practice, non-PIC/S regulators use PIC/S membership as the *proxy* for "trustworthy inspectorate." SAHPRA's Reliance Guideline v5 (VERIFIED) makes this concrete: **"PIC/s Member States"** is listed among the procedures/bodies usable for reliance/collaborative review, alongside WHO CRP (PQ and SRA-approved), ZAZIBONA, EMA MRP/national procedures, Swissmedic **MAGHP**, **AMA** and **EU-M4all**. Note the picscheme.org membership page itself does not spell out certificate-recognition consequences — that mapping lives in each national regulator's reliance guideline.

**Practical rule of thumb for which GMP certificate to get.**
1. **First choice: an EU/EEA GMP certificate published on EudraGMDP**, or a **US FDA**, **UK MHRA**, **TGA**, **Health Canada**, **Swissmedic**, **PMDA** or **MFDS** certificate. These are accepted essentially everywhere, are verifiable online (EudraGMDP self-service verification is what small regulators actually use), and satisfy the "WHO Listed Authority / stringent authority" test simultaneously.
2. **Second: any PIC/S participating authority's certificate** — sufficient for most reliance-based regimes.
3. **Also carry a WHO-format CoPP.** Fiji, CARPHA/CRS and most small regulators ask for the **WHO Model Certificate of a Pharmaceutical Product**, issued by the exporting country's NRA under the WHO Certification Scheme (VERIFIED — Fiji FAQ #7). WHO format ≠ WHO-issued; only WHO Certification Scheme participants may issue one.
4. **For African filings, add WHO PQ or WHO-PQ of the API** where available — SAHPRA recognises WHO Listed Authorities and CPQ (Confirmation of WHO API Prequalification) as reliance anchors.
5. **For the Pacific, an ARTG or Medsafe entry is worth more than any certificate** — Fiji's portal pre-populates from them.
6. **Do not** rely on a non-PIC/S, non-WLA national GMP certificate (Georgia, Armenia, Albania, Barbados, Seychelles, Samoa, Botswana) as your primary evidence — those are markets you file *into*, not certificates you file *with*.

## 7. EU-M4all (Article 58 of Regulation (EC) No 726/2004)

**What it is.** A mechanism whereby **EMA gives a CHMP scientific opinion, in cooperation with WHO, on medicines for human use intended exclusively for markets outside the EU.** Renamed from "Article 58" to **EU-Medicines4all (EU-M4all)** in **September 2017**. Primary sources: EMA overview page (VERIFIED — https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/human-regulatory-overview/marketing-authorisation/medicines-use-outside-european-union), procedural page (VERIFIED — .../obtaining-maintaining-scientific-opinion-medicine-use-outside-european-union), and the definitive Q&A: **EMA/534107/2008 Rev. 5, dated 12 December 2025** (VERIFIED — I fetched and read it: https://www.ema.europa.eu/system/files/documents/regulatory-procedural-guideline/pre-post-article-58-scientific-opinions-procedural-advice-users_v2_en.pdf). Contact: **article58@ema.europa.eu**.

**Eligibility — the honest read for an off-patent generic.**
- **Are generics eligible? Yes, formally.** Q16 of the Rev.5 Q&A lists the permissible application types: full (Art 8(3)), **well-established use (Art 10a)**, fixed combination (Art 10b), informed consent (Art 10c), **generic (Art 10(1))**, **hybrid (Art 10(3))**, and similar biological (Art 10(4)). The EMA infographic states plainly that the procedure "is open to innovative new therapies, new chemical and biological medicines, including biosimilars, vaccines and generic medicines" (VERIFIED — I read https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/leaflet/infographic-medicines-use-outside-eu-eu-m4all_en.pdf).
- **But the disease scope is the binding constraint.** Q3 restricts eligibility to products "intended **exclusively for markets outside the European Union**" for "the prevention or treatment of **diseases of major public health interest**," with an indicative list: vaccines in/for the **WHO EPI**; vaccines against a **WHO 'public health priority disease'**; vaccines in a WHO-managed emergency stockpile; and medicines for **WHO target diseases** — HIV/AIDS, malaria, TB, lymphatic filariasis, trachoma, leishmaniasis, schistosomiasis, African trypanosomiasis, onchocerciasis, dengue, Chagas, leprosy and intestinal helminths. The list is "include but are not limited to," so it isn't a closed set — but **a repurposed off-patent generic for a rare disease with no WHO public-health-priority framing is a hard sell**, and eligibility is decided case-by-case by **CHMP in consultation with WHO**. Note also **Q12: EU orphan status does not apply to EU-M4all applications.**
- **You do not need to be established in the EEA** (Q8). Fee-related SME incentives, however, only apply to SMEs established in the EU/EEA (or accessed via an EU/EEA-established SME).

**Procedure and timeline (VERIFIED, Rev.5).**
1. **Eligibility request — free of charge**, via EMA Service Desk → Business Services → Human Regulatory → Pre-Submission Phase-Human → Eligibility Request. Must include justification, epidemiology/disease-burden data, **the list of countries you intend to market in**, a statement you will not market in the EEA, a draft SmPC/product profile, and any accelerated-assessment rationale. EMA consults WHO; **CHMP confirms eligibility taking WHO's position into account.** Eligibility older than **3 years** at time of application may need resubmission; EMA advises re-confirmation ~**9 months** before intended submission.
2. **Letter of intent / rapporteur request: 7 months before intended submission** (rapporteur appointment will not start earlier).
3. **Pre-submission meeting: 6–7 months before submission** (strongly encouraged). Scientific advice: **40 days**, or 70 days with a face-to-face; **scientific advice on paediatric questions is free**; WHO and target-country regulators may participate.
4. **Evaluation: 210 days**, mirroring the centralised procedure. Milestones: **D1** start · **D80** rapporteur/co-rapporteur ARs · **D94** PRAC RMP AR · **D100** comments · **D101–104** PRAC advice (exceptional) · **D115** draft LoQ · **D120 adoption of List of Questions + clock stop** (and, at the latest, adoption of any GMP/GLP/GCP inspection request) · responses at **D121** · **D157** joint response AR (inspection carried out if applicable; optional QRD product-information meeting ~D165) · **D166** PRAC advice for D180 LoOI · **D170** comments deadline · **D180** LoOI/oral-explanation decision, final inspection report · **D181** clock restart/oral explanation · **D194** joint AR on responses · **D200** comments · **D204** updated AR circulated · **by D210 adoption of CHMP scientific opinion and assessment report.**
5. **Accelerated assessment: 150 days or less.** Notify EMA 6–7 months before submission; formal request ≥2–3 months before.
6. **Re-examination** available: notify within 15 days, grounds within 60 days, CHMP decides within 60 days of grounds.
7. **Conditional opinions and opinions under exceptional circumstances** are both available by analogy to the EU frameworks.

**Fees (VERIFIED, Rev.5 Q32 + EMA fees pages).**
- **Eligibility request: free.**
- **Assessment: the same fees as the centralised MA procedure.** Applicable under **Regulation (EU) 2024/568**, in force **1 January 2025** — see **Annex I, Section 13** and the relevant parts of Annexes I and IV. **EMA does not publish a single headline EU-M4all number; the amount is calculated after submission.** Any specific euro figure quoted for the EU-M4all opinion itself should be treated as **UNVERIFIED** (the €51,900 / €73,900 / €98,400 tiers circulating online are the new **scientific advice** tiers, not the opinion fee).
- Fees are also payable for pre-submission activities, re-examination, inspections, Type II variations, extensions and certificates.
- **Two genuine reliefs: annual fees and PSUR assessment fees are NOT payable for EU-M4all products.** Over a product lifecycle that is material.
- **Total or partial fee waivers** may be granted by the Executive Director in exceptional circumstances; SME status is available (EU/EEA establishment required for the incentives).

**What the output is — and is not.**
- It is a **CHMP scientific opinion plus assessment report. There is no Commission Decision and no marketing authorisation** (Q41, VERIFIED).
- Within **2 months** of adoption, EMA publishes a **public assessment report** (EPAR-equivalent), a lay-language summary, the product information and the list of authorised presentations; documents are **English only**. Assessment reports and Q&A are also published following a withdrawal or a negative opinion.
- **No data protection, no market protection, no market exclusivity** — Regulation 726/2004, Directive 2001/83/EC and Regulation 141/2000 protections do **not** apply to EU-M4all opinions (Q23). For an off-patent generic strategy this is neutral; for a would-be first mover it is a real gap.
- **No invented name required** (no Name Review Group); no mock-ups/specimens a priori; no paediatric investigation plan required (Reg. 1901/2006 doesn't apply); an ERA is expected by analogy but can be justified away.
- EMA **issues certificates** for EU-M4all products within **10 working days** (standard) or **2 working days** (urgent) — useful as the CoPP-equivalent for downstream national filings.
- Post-opinion: full RMP and GVP-standard pharmacovigilance obligations, variations assessed by EMA with WHO, PSURs, signal management. **These do not substitute for the reporting obligations of the countries where the product is actually authorised.**
- **Validity, transfer and lapse** are addressed at Q47–49 of the Q&A (I could not read those pages — treat specifics as **UNVERIFIED**).

**How national regulators actually use it — the part that matters.**
1. **WHO Prequalification "alternative listing procedure."** Q2 (VERIFIED) states that **EU-M4all medicines can be included in the WHO Prequalification List through the 'alternative listing procedure' without any further review by the WHO PQ Programme.** That is the single highest-value downstream consequence: an EU-M4all opinion converts into WHO PQ listing, and WHO PQ is in turn the entry ticket to the **WHO Collaborative Registration Procedure** and to most donor procurement. Innovative products may also be eligible for WHO treatment guidelines and EPI inclusion.
2. **Target-country regulators are inside the procedure.** WHO nominates **experts and observers from target-country authorities**; experts act as scientific reviewers of the rapporteurs' assessment reports, observers may attend CHMP; neither votes. You must send WHO experts/observers your CTD Modules 1 and 2 at procedure start and all subsequent responses. This is deliberate: the reviewing regulators are the ones you'll later file with.
3. **Named national reliance.** **SAHPRA's Reliance Guideline v5 (6 Aug 2025) explicitly added "EU-M4all" to its list of procedures usable for reliance/collaborative review** — the document history states the v5 revision existed to "Add AMA and EU-M4all" (VERIFIED, read in the PDF). This is the cleanest documented example of a named African regulator accepting EU-M4all as a reliance basis.
4. **Documented precedent.** The DRC granted national authorisation of **fexinidazole** in December 2018, within about a month of the CHMP positive opinion of 15 November 2018, on the basis of the CHMP assessment.

**Products with EU-M4all / Article 58 opinions.** EMA does not maintain a single clean published "list of Article 58 opinions" page — they are dispersed across the medicines database. What is verifiable:
- **As of July 2020 (VERIFIED, EMA infographic read directly): 11 scientific opinions, 138 approvals granted worldwide, 90 non-EU countries** having authorised an EU-M4all-evaluated medicine. Named products: **Umbipro** (umbilical cord infection, newborns); **Hemoprostol** (post-partum haemorrhage); **Hexaxim** and **Tritanrix HB** (childhood combination vaccines); **Mosquirix** (paediatric malaria vaccine); **Pyramax** (pyronaridine-artesunate, malaria; granules formulation was the first paediatric antimalarial to get a positive opinion); **Aluvia**, **Lamivudine ViiV**, **Lamivudine/Zidovudine ViiV** (HIV); **Dapivirine Vaginal Ring** (HIV PrEP); **Fexinidazole Winthrop** (human African trypanosomiasis). Several are marked as also centrally authorised in the EU.
- EMA's current overview page states **12 positive opinions between 2004 and 2020, leading to 142 authorisations across 91 countries** (VERIFIED) — slightly different vintage to the infographic; both are EMA's own numbers.
- **Post-2020 additions (from search results, primary EMA pages NOT fetched — UNVERIFIED):** **ivermectin/albendazole** for helminth infections (CHMP positive opinion, January 2025) and **Yeytuo (lenacapavir)** long-acting injectable PrEP, reviewed for non-EU countries under EU-M4all in parallel with the EU MAA (July 2025). Q50–52 of the Rev.5 Q&A confirm **parallel EU-M4all + centralised MAA applications are permitted**, with cross-referencing allowed and a shared accelerated-assessment timetable — which is how the lenacapavir dual review was run.

**Gotchas.** (1) "Generics are eligible" is true but nearly irrelevant unless your indication maps to a WHO public-health-priority disease — the eligibility gate is disease-based, not application-type-based. (2) The lead time is long: eligibility → letter of intent at −7 months → pre-submission at −6/−7 months → 210-day evaluation. Budget ~18–24 months from first contact to opinion. (3) You pay full centralised-procedure fees for something that is not an MA. (4) No exclusivity of any kind. (5) You must be able to state you will **not** market in the EEA. (6) The real payoff is the WHO PQ alternative-listing shortcut and the reliance status it confers — value the procedure on that, not on the opinion itself.
