### 3.3 Full / Abridged / Verification — the formal four-pathway scheme is DRAFT only

The **current in-force** framework has no separate codified "abridged" or "verification" pathway — reg. 3 of S.I. 163/2019 provides one application route (Form 1 + CTD Form 2 + sample), and abridgement arises *operationally* through the reliance guideline above.

The **draft** Human Medicines Regulations 2026, reg. 6(1), would codify four pathways (quoted):
- **(a) Full Evaluation Pathway** — NCEs, new biological entities, products not previously approved by any recognised regulatory authority, first-in-class, novel delivery systems/formulations.
- **(b) Abridged Evaluation Pathway** — "products approved by a Stringent Regulatory Authority, World Health Organization Prequalified products, products approved through recognised regional harmonisation initiatives, and **generic products where the reference medicine is registered in Botswana**".
- **(c) Verification Pathway** — "products approved by **two or more** Stringent Regulatory Authorities and products on the **World Health Organization Essential Medicines List** approved by recognised regulatory authorities".
- **(d) Notification Pathway** — low-risk products per guidelines.

reg. 6(5): in applying Abridged and Verification, the Authority may rely on assessments/inspections/decisions of recognised authorities under **s.109 of the Act**, "while retaining sovereign decision-making authority". Draft **Schedule 3** sets Major variations 90 / Minor 60 / Notifications 30 **working days**, but for all *registration* pathways says only **"As per guidelines"** (clock stops during applicant response) — i.e. the G01 guideline continues to govern registration timing.

Statutory reliance hooks in MRSA 2025: **s.109** ("The Authority may make or adopt regulatory decisions based on reports, information or decisions made by another regulatory authority or trusted institution"; may enter mutual recognition agreements), **s.110** (laboratory reliance), **s.111** (regulatory harmonisation initiatives). Definitions of "reliance" and "recognition" are in s.2.

### 3.5 Unregistered / named-patient / emergency supply

Under **S.I. 163 of 2019** (this is Botswana's "Section 21" analogue), read directly from the gazette:

- **Reg. 6 — Exemption from registration of medicines for individual patient.** *"A medical practitioner may apply in **Form 4**, upon payment of an application fee set out in Schedule 5 to the Authority to exempt the registration of medicines from outside Botswana, for his or her patient's personal use."* The application *"shall comply with the guidelines and **shall be signed by an importing pharmacist residing in Botswana**"*. **Validity of the exemption: six months.**
- **Reg. 7 — Import of unregistered medicines for personal use.** Not more than **one month's** supply; between one and three months requires a certified copy of the prescription on request; subsequent supplies require a reg. 6 exemption.
- **Reg. 8 — Exemption from registration of medicines for wholesale.** *"An applicant may apply to the Authority to exempt the registration of medicines for wholesale from outside Botswana **under special circumstances as determined by the Authority**."* **Form 5** + fee. *"The applicant **may be required to pay for the inspection of the manufacturing site** prior to authorisation."* — **this is the commercially relevant unregistered-supply route for a company.**
- **Reg. 9 — Exemption of donated unregistered medicines** — Form 6.

Processing: **48 working hours** (guideline §6.B1.5.1). Carried forward in MRSA 2025 **s.35(4)–(7)** (Authority "may, in such special circumstances as it considers appropriate exempt, in writing, a regulated product"; the applicant remains "accountable for its quality, safety and efficacy on the supply chain") and **s.38** (Registration during public health emergency — Authority may deviate from registration procedures or waive fees).

An **Emergency Assessment Pathway (EAP)** was described on the archived ZAZIBONA page (requires a WHO-declared PHEIC or a Botswana-declared PHE with no licensed product, cGMP compliance, and commitment to complete development; assessed via WHO EUL procedure + BoMRA guidelines). **UNVERIFIED on the current site.**

## 5. LOCAL RESPONSIBLE PERSON / LOCAL AGENT

**Answer: yes under the 2025 Act and the draft 2026 regulations; not explicitly under the 2019 regulations — and in practice foreign entities currently hold Botswana registrations directly.**

**MRSA 2025, s.36 — "Applicant to have local presence"** (quoted verbatim):
> **36. (1) An application for a regulated product shall be made by a company registered, licensed or operating in Botswana.**
> **(2) Where the applicant is not resident in Botswana, the applicant shall appoint a local technical representative or authorised representative who shall be resident or incorporated in Botswana.**
> (3) The appointment for a local technical representative shall be in such manner as may be prescribed.
> (4) The local technical representative or authorised representative shall be qualified as may be prescribed.

**Draft Human Medicines Regulations 2026, reg. 7(2)–(3)** (quoted verbatim):
> (2) An applicant who is not resident or incorporated in Botswana shall appoint a local technical representative who — (a) **is a natural person ordinarily resident in Botswana or a company incorporated in Botswana**; (b) is responsible for all communications with the Authority; (c) ensures compliance with regulatory requirements; (d) receives and processes safety reports and complaints; (e) facilitates inspections and information requests; and (f) has authority to act on behalf of the marketing authorisation holder.
> (3) The appointment ... shall be **in writing, signed by both parties, filed with the Authority before or with the application, and updated within fourteen days of any change.**

Reg. 7(1)(b) makes "proof of appointment of a local technical representative" part of the required administrative documentation.

**Under the currently in-force instruments** the requirement is weaker: S.I. 163/2019 reg. 3(6) says only *"A marketing authorisation holder shall be responsible for the importation, advertising and promotion of his or her medicine."* The Registration Quality Guideline requires a **letter of authorisation for the responsible person** to communicate with BoMRA (Module 1.2.2.2) and proof of registration of a Responsible Pharmacist/Suitably Qualified Person **only for local manufacturers** (Module 1.7.4).

**Empirical check against the live register** (5 600 registered/compliant records): a `localtechnical_representative` field is populated on **3 037 of 5 600 (54%)**. Registrant countries: **South Africa 3 360, India 963, Botswana 326, Canada 204, Namibia 129, Zimbabwe 63, Germany 36.** Most-used local technical representatives include Apotex Inc (204), Pharma Vision (Pty) Ltd (204), Medswana (Pty) Ltd (184), Novartis (157), Cipla Medpro (128). **So today a foreign company can and does hold the registration itself.** Under the 2025 Act that changes — plan on appointing a Botswana-incorporated local technical representative.

## 8. GMP

**Standard applied: WHO GMP.** The GMP guideline's reference annexure cites **WHO Technical Report Series annexes throughout** (TRS 1010 Annex 10 stability, TRS 1010 Annexes 1–2 herbals, TRS 1044/1010 Annex 5 medicinal gases, TRS 902 Annex 9 packaging, TRS 1025 Annex 7 storage & distribution, TRS 1060 Annex 4 blood establishments) and states manufacturers may use any reference engineering texts "that help them attain the **WHO cGMP** Compliance". **It does not adopt the EU GMP Guide or the PIC/S GMP Guide as its standard.**

**Certificates accepted / desk review vs. physical inspection** — from BOMRA/IL/IL/P08/G02 Iss 2.0 (eff 23/04/2026), quoted:

§6.2.1 — BoMRA may rely on GMP inspections by recognised authorities for the same site/block/production line/dosage form, *"This reliance is **unilateral and is implemented through desk assessment** of inspection reports issued by:"*
- **(a) WHO-Listed Authorities as published and periodically updated by the WHO**
- **(b) Regulatory authorities within the SADC region** — *"South Africa Health Products Regulatory Authority, Medicines Control Authority of Zimbabwe, Zambia Medicines Regulatory Authority, and Tanzania Medicines and Medical Devices Authority"* — including outcomes from **WHO Prequalification** and **ZAZIBONA**
- **(c) Other regulatory authorities e.g. Ghana FDA**

§6.3.1 — **Recognition** (unilateral) of GMP outcomes from the **WHO Prequalification Programme** and from **ZAZIBONA and AMA**. §6.3.2 — facilities approved through these *"may not routinely require an onsite inspection"*.

§6.2.2 — desk assessment requires **complete, accurate and unredacted**: inspection reports; reviewed CAPA plans + evidence of implementation; valid GMP certificates; **APQR reports**; relevant batch manufacturing and packaging records.

Duration/limits:
- §6.2.4 WLA-jurisdiction sites: continued desk reliance if a valid GMP certificate is maintained, routine WLA inspections **"generally not exceeding three (3) to four (4) years"**, and no evidence of non-compliance.
- §6.2.5 SADC/other-authority sites: inspection and approval **not older than two (2) years**.
- §6.2.7 GMP clearance granted by desk assessment is valid for **the referenced certificate's validity or two (2) years, whichever is shorter**.
- ⚠️ **§6.2.8 — "For manufacturing facilities located outside WLA jurisdictions, reliance shall be limited to a maximum of two (2) consecutive cycles. Thereafter, an on-site inspection shall be conducted."** Budget for a physical inspection of an Indian/Chinese CMO by roughly the third cycle.
- §6.2.3 BoMRA reserves the right to require **on-site or remote (virtual)** inspection based on risk. A "Guidelines for Performing Remote Inspection" (IL-IL-P18-G01 Iss 1.0) exists on the site.
- §6.2.10 Reliance is **refused** where inspection scope is irrelevant, there are unresolved critical/major deficiencies, documentation is incomplete/unreliable/**redacted**, there are data-integrity concerns, or there is evidence of significant regulatory action/recalls/quality defects.
- §6.1.4(b) In assessing a reference authority BoMRA considers **"Preferably, WHO GBT Maturity Level 3 and above"** and participation in ZAZIBONA/AMA/WLA frameworks.

**Dossier GMP evidence** (Quality Guideline Module 1.7): list of manufacturers', packers' and Finished Product Release Controls' names and licence numbers with **dates of inspection by the Health Authorities of ICH, PIC/S and SADC**; **inspection reports not older than three years**; **latest GMP certificate not older than three years** for manufacturers, packers and FPRCs. Module 1.7 preamble: *"For all medicines, irrespective of the country of origin, it is expected that key manufacturing and/or processing steps ... are performed in plants of acceptable standards (see **WHO GMP Guideline**)."*

**PIC/S status — VERIFIED NEGATIVE.** `https://picscheme.org/en/members` — **VERIFIED** (fetched). **Botswana/BoMRA is not a PIC/S participating authority, applicant or pre-accession candidate.** The only African PIC/S member is South Africa's SAHPRA (acceded July 2007). Consistent with this, the GMP reliance guideline's revision log records *"Removal of recognition on Regulatory authorities that participate in the Pharmaceutical Inspection Convention and Pharmaceutical Inspection Cooperation Scheme (PIC/S). This was part of the SRA definition."* — i.e. PIC/S membership per se is **no longer** an independent basis for GMP recognition at BoMRA; WLA status is.

**GMP fees:** desk review **USD 3 500**; on-site by region USD 3 500 (SADC) / 5 000 (rest of Africa) / 6 500 (Asia) / 7 000 (rest of world).

## 11. GOTCHAS — ranked by how much they can hurt you

1. ⚠️ **BoMRA has a declared human-medicines assessment BACKLOG.** Stakeholder Notice, **8 July 2026** — target clearance dates: **New applications → January 2027**; variations filed 2022 → Aug 2026, 2023 → Oct 2026, 2024–25 → Jan 2027; renewals → Jan 2027. BoMRA explicitly asks applicants to *"Utilise reliance and recognition pathways where applicable"* and to share existing approvals from **"WHO Listed Authorities (WLAs), Tanzania, Zambia, South Africa, Zimbabwe and Namibia"*. **Treat the published 18-month standard timeline as aspirational and the 3-month reliance timeline as the only realistic route in the near term.** `https://www.bomra.co.bw/stakeholder-notice-implementation-of-the-human-medicines-assessment-backlog-clearance-plan/` — **VERIFIED** (200)
2. ⚠️ **Legal-basis uncertainty.** Confirm in writing which Act is in force (2013 No. 8 vs 2025 No. 29) and whether any 2026 SI has been gazetted. Fees, the local-representative rule and the pathway taxonomy all differ between the two regimes.
3. ⚠️ **Fee regime change is coming and is expensive for foreign applicants.** Today an orphan registration is **BWP 1 000** and there is no USD-denominated retention fee. The draft moves you to **USD** pricing with an **USD 800/year retention fee** and USD 4 000 five-yearly renewal. If the orphan route is viable, filing under the current schedule is worth a great deal.
4. **CPP is mandatory.** Module 1.10.2: *"A copy of the WHO-type Certificate of a Pharmaceutical product should be submitted in this section."* Applies to **all applications including multisource/generics**. BoMRA issues its own CPP for **BWP 250**.
5. **Registration in country of origin is not strictly required — but you must explain.** Module 1.10.1 requires a list of every country where the same product has been **submitted, approved, rejected or withdrawn**, with dates, and **reasons for rejection or withdrawal**. *"If no application has been submitted for registration in the country of origin, include a statement to provide the reason for this decision."* Module 1.10.3/1.10.4 require copies of registration certificates/MAs and the foreign PI/SmPC/PIL from ICH/SADC/other recognised NMRAs.
6. **Samples.** One sample **in each of the proposed packaging materials**, plus a **CoA whose batch number matches the sample**. Required only after screening is passed. More may be requested.
7. **Language/labelling.** All forms **in English**. PIL *"written in layman's language (Basic English)"*. Labels *"prepared as per **SADC guideline on product information and labelling**"*. PI must comply with local Botswana requirements including **schedule and registration numbers**. Mock-ups or specimens for **all strengths and pack sizes**. Braille section is marked "For future use."
8. **Annual retention is a live cancellation risk and it is public.** Reg. 4: registration is valid **five years subject to annual submission of information accompanied by a fee**. The BRIMS public register openly exposes retained vs **unretained** status — currently **5 600 unretained** vs **429 retained** records. Competitors and partners can see it.
9. **Renewal deadline is hard.** Reg. 5(2)(c): renewal application must be submitted **not later than six months before the expiry date** of registration.
10. **Query clock.** Max **3 months** to respond; blow it and the application is **rejected/closed**; restarting costs a **new fee**, a new reference number, and an independent decision. Expedited fee applies again if you want speed on the refile.
11. **Reliance sameness rule.** Your Botswana dossier must be **identical to the most updated product on record at the reference authority**, with all post-approval variation letters. Divergent local formulations/packs break the pathway.
12. **GMP reliance is capped.** Two consecutive desk-assessment cycles for non-WLA-jurisdiction sites, then a physical inspection (USD 5 000–7 000 for Africa/Asia/rest-of-world).
13. **Multi-strength filings must include the BE strength** (G01 §4.1.12).
14. **Ad valorem import fees.** **0.15%** of consignment value for ordinary products; **0.25%** for wholesale-exempted products — scales with your COGS, unlike the flat permit fee.
15. **MA holder owns importation, advertising and promotion** (reg. 3(6)) — that liability travels with the registration.
16. **BoMRA may publish assessment-report summaries for both approved and rejected applications** (reg. 3(5)). A rejection can become public.
17. **Payment reference discipline.** Only the **11-digit BRIMS quotation number** is accepted as a payment reference; anything else and the payment fails and the service is not rendered.
18. **Reg. 8 wholesale exemption may trigger a site inspection at your cost** *before* authorisation — the "quick" unregistered-import route is not always quick.

## 12. RECOMMENDED PLAY FOR AN OFF-PATENT REPURPOSING PORTFOLIO

1. Query the BRIMS public API first (it is open, no auth) to check whether each candidate molecule is already registered in Botswana, by whom, and via which local technical representative — that determines whether you get the draft "generic where the reference medicine is registered in Botswana" abridged route.
2. Secure a **WLA or ZAZIBONA-member approval first** (South Africa/SAHPRA is the highest-leverage single approval — it is both a ZAZIBONA active member and WHO ML3). Then file at BoMRA under reliance for the **3-month** timeline instead of 18.
3. Apply for **orphan designation before or with** the application. Under the current schedule that is **BWP 1 000**; under the draft it is a 50% cut on registration and retention plus priority access to reliance pathways and a 50%-of-full-evaluation target timeline.
4. Line up a Botswana-incorporated **local technical representative** now (use the Licensed Facilities XLSX); it is already normal practice and becomes mandatory under MRSA 2025 s.36.
5. Write to **registration@bomra.co.bw** to confirm, in one message: (a) which Act/SI is currently in force; (b) the exact **screening fee** (the gazette misprint); (c) ZAZIBONA submission windows and reference-country assignment; (d) whether eCTD is accepted.
