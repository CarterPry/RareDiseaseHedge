## How to read this

The **orphan-flagged, multi-regulator, INN** candidates are the highest-value rows: an INN exists (mature molecule), more than one stringent regulator approved it (real drug, real dossier), and at least one called it orphan (already in rare-disease territory). Start manual review there.

- **They may still be approved somewhere unseen.** Only EMA *centralised*, Health Canada, Swissmedic and PMDA-FY are loaded, so a drug licensed in Japan, Australia, or via an EU national/mutual-recognition route lands here wrongly. Known cases are flagged AUTHORISED_ELSEWHERE; that list is hand-built and certainly incomplete. Wiring in EMA Article 57 and a Japanese source is what actually fixes this.

### Known residual error classes (do not skip)

- **US OTC-monograph marketing is NOT a disqualifying approval.** s.529(a)(4)(B)(i)(I) turns on a prior approval *in an application under 505(b)(1), (b)(2) or (j)*. A drug marketed under an OTC monograph has no such application, so it does not bar a voucher. The exclusion set is therefore correctly NDA/ANDA-based, and adding OTC-monograph moieties to it would introduce false NEGATIVES. (The earlier version of this note claimed the opposite and used aspirin as the example; aspirin also holds NDAs, so it was doubly wrong.) What OTC status does change is commercial: no exclusivity runway and existing competition. openFDA `drug/ndc` `marketing_category` is the field that tells them apart.

- **Pre-1962 approvals may not count against a candidate.** In the *Sun Pharma* litigation over phenobarbital (SEZABY) the D.D.C. held that a pre-1962 NDA which became effective by FDA inaction and was later 'deemed approved' was never *approved* in the ordinary sense, so it did not bar a rare-pediatric voucher. 740 applications in this Drugs@FDA snapshot have an original approval before 10 Oct 1962, and 70 distinct active moieties are approved ONLY through them. This diff still excludes all 70. If that holding stands, they are a separate candidate population rather than exclusions — a district-court ruling, so treat it as a live legal theory, not settled law.

- **Pre-2000 US withdrawals are missing from the bulk files.** Of 30 hand-checked drugs with genuine FDA approvals later withdrawn, 13 are absent from Drugs@FDA + Orange Book — every one withdrawn before ~2000 (terfenadine, astemizole, mibefradil, temafloxacin, dexfenfluramine, encainide, flosequinan, zomepirac, benoxaprofen, ticrynafen, nomifensine, methaqualone, phenformin). Post-2000 withdrawals are retained. A withdrawn approval still disqualifies, so this is a real false-positive channel; measured 2026-08-29, none of the 13 currently appears in any loaded foreign register, so the hole is latent rather than active. `US_WITHDRAWN_SUPPLEMENT` patches one case (betahistine) and is not a substitute for a 21 CFR 216.24 feed.

- **Prodrug/conjugate exclusion runs via GSRS** (two hops along ACTIVE MOIETY / PRODRUG / SALT-PARENT edges) and catches the isavuconazole-class leaks the string layer measured. It depends on the GSRS graph being complete for the substance; a missing edge is a missing exclusion. Two such gaps are known and handled explicitly: the stereoisomer layer (GSRS holds no enantiomer/racemate edge for zopiclone/eszopiclone or enclomifene/clomifene) and `GSRS_GAP_PRODRUGS` (tegomil fumarate -> monomethyl fumarate). Newly-INN'd substances are the likeliest next gap, so prodrug-looking names still deserve a manual glance.

- **EMA is centralised-only, and that cuts both ways.** Nationally-authorised EU drugs are absent, so a 'never-US' moiety may in fact be approved via a member state — and, more damagingly, a drug whose only centralised record is a refusal gets filed as dossier-only when it holds a national, Japanese or Australian licence. `KNOWN_AUTHORISED_ELSEWHERE` hand-corrects six such cases; the real fix is the EMA Article 57 (xEVMPD) product file, which carries all nationally-authorised EEA products and measured 377 additional named never-US moieties on 2026-08-29.

- **Japan is effectively absent, and Switzerland nearly so.** Only PMDA FY2025 (140 rows) is loaded and 139 of them fail to resolve, so Japan contributes 1 moiety. Swissmedic resolves 102 of 2,970 rows (3%) because the list is in Latin pharmacopoeial genitives ('abacaviri sulfas'). The unresolved mass is Swiss, not Japanese — 2,868 rows against 139. KEGG DRUG (rest.kegg.jp/list/drug) carries Japanese drugs under Latin-script names with JAN tags and is the cheapest route into Japan without building a katakana transliteration layer.

- This diff does NOT test the § 360ff(a)(3) rare-pediatric gate, FTO, or existing orphan exclusivity. `pipeline/eligibility.py` walks all ten statutory criteria per candidate and labels which a human must decide; it never returns 'eligible', only 'nothing has ruled it out yet'.
