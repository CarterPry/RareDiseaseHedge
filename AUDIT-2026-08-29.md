# Accuracy audit — 2026-08-29

**Scope:** full adversarial audit of the Funnel-A pipeline (code, data, statutory
encoding, dashboard, docs) plus an exhaustive sweep for data sources not yet
wired in. 10 independent auditors raised 78 findings; each was put to 3
independent verifiers on different lenses (correctness / materiality /
primary-source), giving 234 verdicts, of which 33 refuted the finding. Every
defect acted on below was then re-measured by hand against the real files.

**Bottom line: the previous headline number was wrong by roughly half, and the
reason was a single unused column.** `load_ema` read EMA's "Medicine status"
field and never filtered on it, so applications that were *withdrawn, refused,
or still pending an EC decision* were counted as foreign approvals. 33 of the 67
"approved abroad, never approved in the US" candidates had never been authorised
anywhere the pipeline could see — including 11 of the 13 orphan-flagged rows the
dashboard presented as the highest-value leads. Two independent measures agree
exactly: 33 of the 57 EMA-sourced candidates carry no marketing-authorisation
date, and 33 trace only to non-authorising status values.

A second, independent error is narrower than it first looks but worth stating
precisely: **`eligibility.py` encodes superseded law, and contradicts the
README.** It asserts the application may be filed under "505(b)(1) or
505(b)(2)"; Pub. L. 117-9 (2021) rewrote § 360ff(a)(4)(B) to require the
application be "the subject of an application submitted under section
355(b)(1)" — a full 505(b)(1) NDA. To be clear about where the repo stands:
`README.md` has this **right** ("505(b)(2) is not 505(b)(1)", "Drug approved
abroad… via full 505(b)(1) NDA | YES"), as does `rdh_diff.py`. The thesis
documentation is sound; the module that scores eligibility per candidate was the
one carrying the pre-2021 text, which is the worst place for it to be.

## Critical findings

**C1 — `load_ema` never filtered on authorisation status.**
`pipeline/sources.py:84-119`. The EMA medicines report is an EPAR index, not an
approval list: of 2,290 human rows, 312 carry a status that means the product
never held an authorisation (266 "Application withdrawn", 63 "Refused", 29
"Opinion", 2 "Withdrawn from rolling review", 1 "Opinion under re-examination"),
and a further 412 were authorised and later lost the licence. All were ingested
identically. Corroborated two ways: exactly 33 of 57 EMA-sourced named
candidates have an empty marketing-authorisation date (col 31).
*Fixed:* every row is labelled `authorised` / `previously_authorised` /
`never_authorised`; the text status is authoritative and the EC decision date is
used only to classify an unrecognised status, so a newly-added status value
cannot silently become an approval. Never-authorised rows are kept in a separate
`funnelA_dossier_only.csv` rather than discarded — the dossier and human data are
real, and that is the feasibility signal the thesis actually rests on.

**C2 — `eligibility.py` encodes pre-2021 law and contradicts the README.**
`pipeline/eligibility.py`. The docstring and the `filed_as_505b1_or_505b2`
criterion asserted the application may be filed under 505(b)(1) *or* 505(b)(2).
§ 360ff(a)(4)(B)(i)(II) requires 355(b)(1) only, per Pub. L. 117-9 (2021); the
pre-2021 text is what most law-firm explainers still describe, and the vouchers
awarded on 505(b)(2) applications all predate it. `README.md` and
`rdh_diff.py` both state the rule correctly, so this was an internal
contradiction in which the wrong version sat in the module that scores
per-candidate eligibility.
*Fixed:* criterion renamed `filed_as_505b1`, cited to (a)(4)(B)(i)(II), with the
public-law history in the note. Consequence for the thesis is unchanged from what
the README already says — a 505(b)(1) may rest on published literature the
sponsor did not generate, but it is a full NDA with the cost and data burden that
implies.

**C3 — the statutory test is "active moiety", not "active ingredient".**
Same file. The encoded criterion quoted the pre-2021 "active ingredient,
including any ester or salt" formulation and cited 505(b)(1)/(b)(2)/(j) *and*
351(a)/351(k) together. The current drug prong at (a)(4)(B)(i)(I) says "no active
moiety (as defined by the Secretary in section 314.3 of title 21, Code of Federal
Regulations)" and lists only 505(b)(1)/(b)(2)/(j); 351(a)/(k) belong to the
separate biologic prong at (a)(4)(B)(ii).
This one is good news for the implementation: **the pipeline screens on active
moiety, which is exactly the correct standard.** The code was right and its own
documentation understated it.

**C4 — UNII column indices were positional, and FDA moved a column.**
`pipeline/unii.py`. `load_records` read `SUBSTANCE_TYPE` at fixed index 23. The
**4 Aug 2026** UNII release inserted a new `UTF8_DISPLAY_NAME` column at that
position, shifting `SUBSTANCE_TYPE` to 24. `row[23]` therefore returned a display
name, `is_biologic()` stopped matching, and the biologic/mixture filter
**collapsed from 203 rows to 4 — leaking ~197 proteins and mixtures straight
into the candidate list, which jumped from 32 to 114 while every intermediate
count still looked plausible.**

This was found only because fixing H4 caused a real refresh; on the stale
Feb-2026 file the bug was invisible. It is the most dangerous shape of defect in
this codebase — a routine upstream data refresh silently inverting a filter — and
it would have shipped as a 3.5× "improvement" in candidate count.
*Fixed three ways:* (1) columns in both `load_records` and `load_names` are now
resolved **by header name**, case- and separator-insensitively, so the same shift
cannot recur (it also absorbs the `Display Name` → `DISPLAY_NAME` rename in the
same release); (2) positional indices remain only as a fallback and warn loudly
when used; (3) `_check_subtypes` validates the *shape* of the parsed column —
a small controlled vocabulary dominated by `chemical` — and warns if that stops
being true, which catches the class even if header names change too.

Verified across both releases: 7 distinct substance types and ~42,000 biologics
in each. With the fix, the fresh 4 Aug 2026 index reproduces **the same 32
candidates** as the Feb index — a useful stability result in its own right.

Two further instances of the same root cause, both fixed:
`_snapshot_label()` reported the mtime of the hardcoded Feb file rather than the
index actually loaded, and `test_accuracy.py --real` loaded the hardcoded Feb
files — so the harness that exists to "replay labeled cases through the SAME
code path" was testing a different identity index from the pipeline.

**C5 — all five (a)(4) citations were wrong.**
`eligibility.py` cited a structure `(a)(4)(A)(i)`–`(a)(4)(A)(v)` that does not
exist in the statute. Correct: exclusion test (a)(4)(B)(i)(I); 505(b)(1) filing
(a)(4)(B)(i)(II); priority review (C); pediatric data (D); no adult indication
(E). *Fixed*, and the missing (a)(4)(F) "approved after 30 September 2016"
criterion was added so the encoded set matches the statute rather than a
remembered subset of it.

## High / medium findings

**H1 — two stereoisomer false positives the GSRS layer structurally cannot see.**
`ZOPICLONE` is the racemate of **eszopiclone** (Lunesta, NDA 021476) and
`ENCLOMIFENE` is a component of US-approved **clomifene citrate** (Clomid). Both
were in the headline list with no flag, and the REVIEW bucket was empty across
all 94 survivors. Verified live: FDA's own GSRS graph carries **no**
enantiomer/racemate edge for either pair (eszopiclone's record links to zopiclone
only via `IMPURITY->PARENT`), and `has_stereo_marker` misses them because the
descriptor is fused into the INN stem rather than written as "(R)-"/"(±)-".
*Fixed:* `moiety.stereo_name_variants` adds/strips WHO INN stereo prefixes
(es-, lev-, dex-, ar-, en-, zu-, cis-, trans-, rac-…) in both directions, and
first strips a trailing descriptor so UNII's "ZOPICLONE, (R)-" spelling is
treated as ZOPICLONE. That last part is load-bearing: which UNII record claims a
moiety key is **not stable across UNII releases** — the 4 Aug 2026 file made
"ZOPICLONE" resolve to the (R)- enantiomer record instead of the racemate, which
moved the row into the unnamed appendix and silently dropped its flag until the
suffix handling was added. Over the full survivor set the layer now finds three
real pairs (zopiclone/eszopiclone, enclomifene/clomifene, levomenthol/menthol)
and raises no false alarms — checked against near-miss shapes like "VITAMIN E"
and "INTERFERON BETA". Verdict is REVIEW, not exclusion: whether a single
enantiomer of an approved racemate shares the approved active moiety under
21 CFR 314.3 is genuinely unsettled.

**H2 — `TEGOMIL FUMARATE` is a prodrug of a US-approved moiety.**
Tegomil fumarate (Riulvy, EMA-authorised 2026) is two monomethyl-fumarate
moieties joined by tetraethylene glycol, cleaved in vivo to **monomethyl
fumarate** — US-approved as Bafiertam. GSRS holds no relationship at all for
UNII MXD6KMG2ZP, so the graph layer could not catch it.
*Fixed:* a small, cited `GSRS_GAP_PRODRUGS` table that re-verifies the named
parent against the live US record at run time, so a stale entry can never assert
an approval that no longer exists.

**H3 — two matcher gaps, found by using EMA Article 57 as an unseen test set.**
- `DABIGATRAN` did not meet US `DABIGATRAN ETEXILATE MESYLATE` (Pradaxa):
  `ETEXILATE` was missing from the ester-prodrug suffix list that already held
  AXETIL, PROXETIL, PIVOXIL, MEDOXOMIL and friends.
- INN `CEFALEXIN` did not meet USAN `CEPHALEXIN` (different UNIIs, different
  keys). Measured across 26 known INN/USAN divergences, UNII synonyms unify all
  of them (paracetamol/acetaminophen, salbutamol/albuterol,
  ciclosporin/cyclosporine, rifampicin/rifampin…) **except** the cephalosporin
  CEF-/CEPH- rule, which cost 2 misses (cefalexin, cefradine).
*Fixed:* `ETEXILATE` added; a narrow leading-CEF/CEPH orthographic bridge added.
Both now resolve; cefamandole, cefovecin and all 32 candidates are unaffected.

**H4 — `fetch.py` downloaded the UNII index but never extracted it, and the diff
hardcoded a six-month-old filename.**
FDA date-stamps these files. `fetch.py` wrote `UNIIs.zip` while `rdh_diff.py`
read `UNII_Names_26Feb2026.txt`, a name `fetch.py` never produced — so a
refreshed identity index could never reach the pipeline, which went on reading a
Feb-2026 map while reporting a current snapshot. The newest release is
**4 Aug 2026**, 159 days newer.
*Fixed:* the zips are extracted after download, and the diff globs for the
newest date-stamped file by parsing the embedded date (string sort would put
26Feb2026 after 4Aug2026). The index is now 4 Aug 2026 — **and doing this
immediately exposed C4**, a positional-column bug that the stale file had been
hiding.

> **Audit self-correction worth recording.** I first concluded these URLs were
> blocked, having tested with `curl -I`. They are served through an AWS API
> Gateway that accepts **GET only** and answers HEAD with
> `403 MissingAuthenticationToken`. A verifier caught the error; a plain GET
> retrieves 20.3 MB and 14.5 MB fine. The 403 was entirely an artifact of the
> test method.

**H5 — `fetch.py` disabled TLS verification for every host.**
`ssl.CERT_NONE` plus `check_hostname = False`, justified in a comment as "some
regulator hosts have incomplete chains". Re-tested all seven hosts
(ema.europa.eu, fda.gov, canada.ca, swissmedic.ch, precision.fda.gov,
gsrs.ncats.nih.gov, download.open.fda.gov): **all verify cleanly**. The bypass
bought nothing and allowed silent substitution of every input to the thesis.
*Fixed:* full verification; all sources still download.

**H6 — the Swissmedic URL had been dead for some time.**
404, so every "refresh" silently kept the stale local copy — the atomic-write
design meant this never surfaced as an error. Swissmedic renamed the list.
*Fixed:* current path (`zugelassen_wirkstoffe…/Stoffliste.xlsx`, "Stand vom
31.07.2026"), scraped from the published index.

**H7 — every foreign loader returned `[]` silently when its file was missing.**
A missing download turned into a smaller candidate list that looked exactly as
confident; losing EMA alone would drop most of the headline set with no warning.
*Fixed:* each loader's expected filename is named and a zero-row result is a loud
warning.

**H8 — Japan and Switzerland are effectively absent, and the report blamed the
wrong one.** The funnel described 3,605 unresolved rows as "mostly PMDA
katakana". Measured per source: **Swissmedic 2,868 of 2,970 (97%)**, PMDA 139 of
140 (99%), Health Canada 223 of 715 (31%), EMA 375 of 2,292 (16%). Japan
contributes **1** resolved moiety; the unresolved mass is Swiss Latin
pharmacopoeial genitives ("abacaviri sulfas"), outnumbering Japan 20:1.
*Fixed:* the funnel table now reports unresolved counts per source. This also
explains `multi_regulator: 0` — a real result, not a bug: EMA here is
centralised-only while Canada and Switzerland list mostly nationally-approved
drugs, so the sets barely overlap. The dashboard now says so instead of silently
scoring every candidate down for it.

**H9 — pre-2000 US withdrawals are missing from the bulk files.**
Of 30 hand-checked drugs with genuine FDA approvals later withdrawn, **13 are
absent** from Drugs@FDA + Orange Book — every one withdrawn before ~2000
(terfenadine, astemizole, mibefradil, temafloxacin, dexfenfluramine, encainide,
flosequinan, zomepirac, benoxaprofen, ticrynafen, nomifensine, methaqualone,
phenformin). Post-2000 withdrawals are retained (rofecoxib, valdecoxib, cisapride,
sibutramine, cerivastatin, troglitazone all resolve). A withdrawn approval still
disqualifies, so this is a real false-positive channel — but **none of the 13
currently appears in any loaded foreign register**, so the hole is latent, not
active. `US_WITHDRAWN_SUPPLEMENT` patches exactly one case and is not a
substitute for a 21 CFR 216.24 feed. *Documented with the measurement; not
"fixed", because the honest fix is a feed.*

**H10 — the dashboard's PRV price was ~$50-100M low.**
It claimed "recently sold for ~$100–160 million". This repo's own SEC-verified
table (`sources/18`) records 2026 sales at **$180M (Rocket), $195M (Denali),
$205M (Cyprium)** and a 2026-27 planning band of $175–210M.
*Fixed:* the three 2026 SEC-verified figures are now quoted directly.

**M1 — `METHYLPARABEN` and `AMYLMETACRESOL` are not drug candidates.**
Methylparaben is a preservative (its Swissmedic source name is the food-additive
code "E 218"); it passes the "named drug" filter because UNII_Records genuinely
carries a USAN_ID for it (`Y-26`), and openFDA NDC confirms US
`OTC MONOGRAPH FINAL` status. Amylmetacresol is an OTC throat-lozenge antiseptic
with a real INN. Both are correct "never US-approved under an application" results
and useless as repurposing leads — the INN-or-USAN filter cannot separate them.
*Not fixed:* an ATC/therapeutic-class screen is the right instrument, and
inventing a hand-exclusion list here would hide the structural point.

**M2 — the funnel report's OTC-monograph warning stated the law backwards.**
It said a US OTC-monograph moiety "can still surface as a false candidate", using
aspirin as the example. § 360ff bars a prior approval *in an application under
505(b)(1)/(b)(2)/(j)*; OTC-monograph marketing involves no such application and
therefore does **not** disqualify. Adding OTC moieties to the exclusion set would
introduce false *negatives*. (Aspirin also holds NDAs, so the example was doubly
wrong.) *Fixed.*

**M3 — the snapshot label was a hand-edited literal.**
It claimed "EMA 5 Aug 2026", named no Health Canada file at all, and survived a
refresh of `drug_ap.zip`. *Fixed:* derived from the mtimes of the files actually
on disk, and it reports `MISSING` rather than omitting an absent source.

**M4 — "~7 estimated actionable" was a hardcoded, unsourced number** presented as
the honest size of the opportunity. *Removed*, replaced with the derived
dossier-only and REVIEW counts.

## Confirmed accurate (checked, found sound)

Worth as much as the defect list, because these are the things a reader would
otherwise have to take on trust:

- **The US-exclusion side holds up.** Cross-checked all 67 pre-fix candidates
  against the openFDA NDC directory (137,590 records): **zero** are marketed in
  the US under NDA / ANDA / BLA / NDA-authorized-generic. 18 appear only as
  `BULK INGREDIENT`, `EXPORT ONLY` or OTC-monograph — none disqualifying.
- **The EMA and Health Canada column indices are correct**, contrary to initial
  suspicion: EMA 0/3/6/7/8/11/15/23/31 all verified against the live header, and
  Health Canada `drug_ap.txt` col 2 = class, col 1 = product categorization — so
  the vet/OTC screen is genuinely wired to the right fields. (UNII's indices were
  correct for the Feb 2026 file and broke on the Aug release — see C4. Only UNII
  is now header-resolved; EMA and Health Canada remain positional and are the
  obvious next candidates for the same treatment.)
- **`ALFACALCIDOL` correctly stays a candidate.** It is a prodrug of US-approved
  calcitriol, but activation is a 25-hydroxylation — not an ester or salt — so
  under 21 CFR 314.3 it is a distinct active moiety.
- **`TEGOMIL FUMARATE` is a real INN**, not a moiety-extraction artifact (though
  it is a false positive for the separate reason in H2).
- **EUA is not an approval.** Molnupiravir's US EUA does not disqualify it;
  the pipeline's treatment is right.
- **BLA biologics are present in Drugs@FDA Products.txt** — there is no Purple
  Book hole on the exclusion side.
- **Unresolved US ingredient rows are immaterial:** 334 of 117,171 (0.3%), with
  zero overlap against any candidate.
- **The GSRS cache is complete** for its survivor set — 373 entries, no `ok=false`.
- **The sunset is right:** 30 September 2029 per § 360ff(b)(5); program lapsed
  20 December 2024 and was restored by the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026
  (Pub. L. 119-75, 3 February 2026) § 6604. There is no separate designation
  deadline, so encoding a single date is correct.
- **Funnel arithmetic reconciles:** 32 named + 32 dossier-only + 27 unnamed = 91
  small-molecule survivors, with 2 REVIEW counted separately.

Of the 78 findings raised, **33 verdicts refuted the finding they examined** —
including my own UNII conclusion (H4) and several overstated severity claims. The
refutations are the reason this document does not contain them.

### The A2 population is documented but never computed

`README.md` already defines Funnel A as having two sub-populations — **A1**
(never approved in the US) and **A2** (US drugs whose *only* prior approval was a
pre-1962 deemed-approved NDA, newly available per *Sun Pharma v. FDA*, D.D.C.,
over phenobarbital/SEZABY) — with the appropriate caveats about it being an
unreviewed district-court holding. **No code implements A2.** `rdh_diff.py`
excludes any moiety appearing anywhere in Drugs@FDA or the Orange Book,
regardless of approval vintage, so A2 is defined in prose and discarded in
practice.

First quantification, from data already on disk: **740 applications** in the
current Drugs@FDA snapshot have an original approval before 10 Oct 1962, and
**70 distinct active moieties are approved only through them** — trimethadione,
paramethadione, phensuximide, diethylcarbamazine, guanidine, furazolidone and
dimercaprol among them, several squarely rare-and-pediatric. Caveats on the
measurement: it uses the earliest ORIG `SubmissionStatusDate` as the vintage
proxy, many pre-1962 records have blank or placeholder dates, and truly
"deemed approved" drugs are often absent from Drugs@FDA altogether — so 70 is a
noisy floor. Some entries (sulfur, inulin, histamine) are diagnostics or reagents
rather than repurposing candidates.

It costs nothing to compute and is the cheapest new population available, but it
must land in a clearly-labelled speculative bucket, never the headline.

## Residual limitations after all fixes

- The pipeline settles **one** of the ten statutory criteria by machine (the
  active-moiety exclusion test). Rarity and pediatric-onset need external data;
  seriousness, priority review and all three filing-design criteria need a human
  and ultimately FDA. `eligibility.py` can never return "eligible" — only
  "nothing has ruled it out yet", which is the correct ceiling.
- "Never approved in the US" rests on bulk files that demonstrably omit pre-2000
  withdrawals (H9). A hand check remains necessary for any candidate whose
  molecule is old enough to have had a 1980s US life.
- GSRS completeness is assumed. Two gaps are now handled explicitly; newly-INN'd
  substances are the likeliest next one, because curation lags approval.
- Orphan status is **EMA-only**. Absence of the badge means "not checked", not
  "not rare", for every Canadian, Swiss and Japanese row.
- The 2029-09-30 sunset is a hard approval deadline ~37 months out. The repo's
  own optimistic timeline (`sources/26`) already sits on its outer edge with no
  buffer, and its per-program probability model treats correlated risks as
  independent. Treat the timeline, not the candidate count, as the binding
  constraint.
